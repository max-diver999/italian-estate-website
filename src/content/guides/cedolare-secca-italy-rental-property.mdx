## Cedolare Secca Italy: 2026 Rental Property Tax Guide

Quick answer: Cedolare secca is Italy's optional flat tax on residential rental income. In 2026 most foreign landlords pay 21% on gross rent for standard long-term leases and for a first short-term tourist unit, 10% on subsidized concordato leases in designated cities, and 26% on a second short-term property.

The regime replaces progressive IRPEF on that contract but allows no deductions, including IMU. Non-residents may elect it at lease registration if they hold a codice fiscale and meet CIN licensing rules for tourist lets.

Cedolare secca is calculated on gross rent collected under the registered lease during the tax year without subtracting expenses. A non-resident owner registers a Milan 4+4 lease at €1,800 per month, collects €21,600 in 2026, and elects cedolare secca at registration. Italian income tax on that contract equals €21,600 times 21 percent, or €4,536, paid through F24. IMU of roughly €1,200, condominium fees of €2,400, and management at 10 percent, or €2,160, do not reduce the €4,536. Total Italian cash burden on the rental line is therefore €4,536 plus IMU plus operating costs. ## What is cedolare secca on Italian rental property?

Cedolare secca is an optional substitute tax on residential lease income: the landlord pays a flat 21% of gross rent instead of ordinary IRPEF at 23% to 43% plus regional and municipal surcharges. Nothing is deductible, not IMU, not agency fees, not mortgage interest, but nothing is deductible under ordinary IRPEF either, which is what makes the flat rate so widely elected. On €24,000 of annual rent the charge is €5,040, whatever your other income.

Parliament introduced the regime to simplify compliance for small landlords and to pull short-term tourist income into a visible flat-rate channel. For international investors, the main benefit is predictability.

Cedolare secca does not replace IMU, tourist taxes, registration tax on the lease, or home-country income tax for US, UK, EU, or Gulf residents with treaty relief.

|-------|---------------------------|-------|
| IMU second home | Yes | Paid via F24 June and December |
| Tourist tax STR | Yes | Pass-through from guests |
| Home-country tax | Yes | Treaty credit typical |
| IRPEF on same rent | No | Replaced by flat rate |

## What cedolare secca rates apply in 2026?

Three rates operate in 2026. Standard free-market residential leases and one short-let property of your choosing are taxed at 21%. A canone concordato lease in a designated high-tension commune is taxed at 10%. A second short-let property is taxed at 26%. From the third short-let property the activity is presumed to be a business, which means Partita IVA rather than cedolare secca: a threshold that stood at four properties until 2025.

Italy sets cedolare secca rates by contract type and, for tourist lets, by how many short-term properties you operate. The 2026 framework below reflects Decree Law 145/2023 reforms and the January 2026 lowering of the professional host threshold to three units.

Platform operators such as Airbnb and Booking.com withhold 21% on payouts to Italian tax IDs and remit to the Agenzia delle Entrate. If your effective rate is 26% on a second unit, you must top up the difference via F24. ## How does cedolare secca compare to ordinary IRPEF?

The trade is not deductions against simplicity, because a private landlord has no deductions to trade. Ordinary IRPEF taxes 95% of the rent received; IMU, maintenance, management, insurance and mortgage interest reduce nothing. On €30,000 of gross rent, cedolare secca at 21% costs €6,300, while IRPEF on a base of €28,500 costs €6,555 at the bottom 23% band before regional and municipal surcharges push it past €6,900. The flat rate therefore wins even at the lowest bracket, and wins decisively at 35% or 43%.

IRPEF (Imposta sul Reddito delle Persone Fisiche) is Italy's progressive personal income tax. Default rental reporting puts gross rent in the "redditi di fabbricati" category, subtracts a flat 5% abatement plus eligible costs (maintenance, IMU share attributable to letting, some interest), and taxes the net at 23% to 43% national brackets plus regional and municipal surcharges that can add 1 to 2 points.

The comparison is narrower than most guides suggest, because neither regime allows a private landlord to deduct costs. Cedolare secca charges a flat 21% on the rent received. Ordinary IRPEF charges your marginal band on 95% of it: the 5% forfait is the whole allowance, and IMU, maintenance, management, insurance and mortgage interest reduce nothing. At the bottom 23% band that produces roughly 21.85% before regional and municipal surcharges push it past 23%, so the flat rate already wins; at 35% or 43% it wins decisively. Two situations still favour IRPEF and both concern the taxpayer rather than the property: a landlord whose rent is nearly all their Italian income, where personal allowances have room to work, and one carrying renovation detrazione instalments that need IRPEF liability to offset.

Where IRPEF can genuinely win is different, when the rent is close to your only Italian income and personal allowances still have room to work against it.

Stack the same €30,000 on a salary already taxed at 43% and the ordinary regime costs over €12,000 on a base of €28,500, against €6,300 under cedolare secca. That gap is why almost every landlord with other Italian income elects the flat rate at registration.

## When should you elect cedolare secca instead of IRPEF?

Elect it whenever the rent stacks on other Italian income. At the bottom 23% band, IRPEF on 95% of €30,000 costs €6,555 before surcharges against €6,300 flat, and the gap widens sharply at 35% and 43%. The two situations worth modelling both ways are a landlord whose rent is nearly all their Italian income, where personal allowances still have room to work, and one carrying renovation detrazione instalments that need IRPEF liability to absorb.

Elect cedolare secca when flat tax on gross beats progressive tax on net after realistic expenses, and when you value administrative simplicity.

Strong cedolare secca cases in 2026:

1. Standard city lease, light expenses. Milan or Rome long-term let with professional manager at 8 to 12% and moderate IMU. Gross-heavy profile favors 21%.
2. First or second short-let unit with platform withholding and CIN in place. A predictable 21% or 26% on gross beats ordinary IRPEF, which taxes the same gross bookings with no forfait and no cost relief at all.
3. Concordato lease in eligible commune where rent caps apply but 10% cedolare is available. Common in high-demand cities offering concordato inventory.
4. Non-resident with no Italian employment who wants to avoid Modello Redditi PF complexity and audit surface on worldwide reporting to Italy.

Strong IRPEF cases:

1. Major capital expenditure year (new roof, full renovation) where invoices exceed 35 to 40% of gross rent.
2. Low gross rent, high IMU and condominium share relative to collections.
3. Portfolio already in IRPEF with losses carried forward (rare for non-residents but relevant for relocated owners).

Election timing: declare cedolare secca on the registrazione del contratto within the statutory window (generally 30 days from lease start). Switching mid-contract from cedolare to IRPEF is not available; starting IRPEF and later moving to cedolare on the same lease is also blocked once the first annual return path is fixed. New lease, new election.

## What CIN and licensing rules link to cedolare secca?

Any stay under 30 days requires a national CIN issued through the BDSR database, after regional CIR registration where the region operates one, with fines from €800 to €8,000 for a missing code on a listing or a door plaque. Cedolare secca is a tax election and confers no licence: electing it does not make an unregistered let lawful. Get the CIN displayed on every OTA listing and at the entrance before any marketing begins.

National CIN (Codice Identificativo Nazionale): mandatory for stays under 30 days. Obtain through the BDSR portal after regional CIR registration. Display on all OTAs and at the property entrance. Missing CIN: fines €800 to €8,000 plus delisting.

SCIA and business threshold: amateur hosts with one or two STR properties typically skip SCIA in many communes. From 1 January 2026, managing three or more short-term properties triggers professional classification: Partita IVA, SCIA filing, and exit from cedolare secca flat tax benefits.

Safety and guest registration: portable fire extinguishers, smoke and CO detectors, and Alloggiati Web police registration within 24 hours of guest arrival are mandatory under DL 145/2023.

Tourist taxes: municipalities collect imposta di soggiorno separately. Milan STR rates reached €9.50 per person per night in Olympic-corridor zones in 2026. Cedolare secca does not include these amounts.

## How do foreign non-resident owners use cedolare secca?

A landlord living abroad elects the same 21% or 26% rates as an Italian resident, provided they hold a codice fiscale and register each lease with the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days of its start. Tax residency elsewhere does not block the election on Italian-source rent, because Italy taxes the property where it sits.

The home-country overlay (US Schedule E, UK SA105) is applied afterwards, with treaty credit for the Italian tax already paid on F24.

Non-resident landlords may elect cedolare secca on the same terms as Italian residents, provided they hold an Italian codice fiscale and register each lease correctly. Tax residency abroad does not block the flat tax; source taxation on Italian real estate applies regardless.

Step-by-step for foreign owners:

1. Obtain codice fiscale at consulate or Agenzia delle Entrate office before lease registration ([codice fiscale guide](/guides/codice-fiscale-italy-property/) if on site).
2. Open Italian bank account (recommended) for F24 payments and rent collection evidence.
3. Appoint commercialista experienced with non-resident landlords and cross-border clients.
4. Register lease and declare cedolare secca election simultaneously.
5. Obtain CIN before marketing any STR listing.
6. File home-country return with credit for Italian tax paid.

US owners: report Schedule E, claim Foreign Tax Credit for cedolare secca paid. Details in [US tax on Italy rental property](/guides/us-tax-italy-rental-property/).

UK owners: declare SA105, claim double taxation relief for Italian flat tax. See [UK tax on Italy holiday home](/guides/uk-tax-italy-holiday-home/).

EU owners: home-country worldwide reporting continues; treaty credit mechanics vary by state.

Italy does not require a fiscal representative for every non-resident landlord, but banks and platforms increasingly demand a local tax ID and IBAN. Some communes insist on Italian fiscal domicile for bulk STR operators.

## How do you register a lease and pay cedolare secca?

Register the contract with the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days of the start date, using the RLI form, and mark the cedolare secca election on that same filing. Electing the flat tax removes the 2% registration tax and the stamp duty that a standard lease attracts, in exchange for waiving ISTAT rent indexation for the term.

Settle through F24 in the year following collection, and keep the quietanze for five years against Guardia di Finanza cross-checks.

Lease registration (*registrazione*) is the legal trigger. Your commercialista files the contract with the Agenzia delle Entrate, pays imposta di registro (usually 2% of annual rent for long-term leases, tenant-paid on standard forms), and marks the cedolare secca option.

Payment mechanics:

- F24 form with appropriate *codice tributo* for cedolare secca on residential leases
- Annual settlement aligned to rent collection year; advances may apply on STR with high seasonality
- Platform withholding credited against liability on STR income

Keep registration receipt, F24 quietanza, and annual rent ledger for five years minimum. Guardia di Finanza cross-checks OTA data against declared cedolare.

## What operating costs still apply under cedolare secca?

Cedolare secca caps the income tax, not the cost base. IMU at 0.86-1.06% of cadastral value, condominium spese of €100-400 a month, and management at 10-30% of gross all continue to reduce cash flow. What they no longer do is reduce the Italian tax bill, because none of them are deductible.

Commercialista fees run €800 to €2,500 yearly for non-residents. Stacks all lines in [how to calculate rental yield in Italy](/guides/how-to-calculate-rental-yield-italy/) models.

Investors who elect cedolare secca sometimes assume flat tax means all-in taxation. It does not. Budget these recurring lines separately:

- IMU: 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value typical for second homes
- Condominium (*spese condominiali*): €100 to €400+ monthly in cities
- Management: 10 to 15% long-term, 20 to 30% STR gross
- Insurance and maintenance: 1 to 2% of property value annually as reserve
- Tourist tax pass-through and cleaning: STR only
- Commercialista: €800 to €2,500+ yearly for non-resident files

Under cedolare secca, none of the above reduces Italian income tax on rent. They do reduce cash flow and should appear in yield models.

After 21% cedolare, IMU, and 25% STR management, net cash often lands 4 to 6% in Puglia and 2.5 to 4% in Milan long-term stock. Enquiries citing [Italy rental yield guide](/guides/italy-rental-yield-guide/) assumptions without IMU or flat tax are flagged in underwriting.

Insider tip: concordato inventory in Milan and Florence can unlock 10% cedolare but rent caps may reduce gross by 15 to 20% versus free market. Always compare net after cap, not headline rate alone.

## Underwriting snapshot

Four rates cover almost every residential letting in Italy: 21% on a registered long lease, 10% on a qualifying canone concordato, 21% on the first short-let property in a tax year, and 26% on a second. None of them permits a deduction, and from the third short-let property the activity is presumed to be a business.
