## Should you choose short-term or long-term rental by city?

Short-let buys 1.0 to 3.0 gross points over a long lease and costs CIN licensing, cedolare secca at 26% on many second homes, and a seasonal void that in tourism communes can swallow the difference. Milan and Bologna default to furnished twelve-month leases with voids under four weeks — a deliberate trade of headline yield for occupancy certainty. Coastal and island markets reverse that logic.

- Milan and Bologna often reward LTR because corporate and university demand fills winter months.
- Puglia and Sicily reward STR when pool conformità, CIN transfer, and 35% to 50% winter occupancy are modeled honestly.

A yield ranking is only comparable if every city in it is measured the same way, and most published tables are not. Three adjustments make the difference. Gross must be converted to net using each city's actual IMU band at 0.86-1.06% of cadastral value, cedolare secca at 21% or 26%, and management at 10-30% depending on whether the letting is long or short. Vacancy must be modelled locally rather than nationally: four weeks in Milan and five months in a Sicilian resort are both realistic in their own markets. And the regulatory position must be checked at address level, because a licence freeze or a condominium ban removes the higher-yielding route entirely. Applied consistently, those three adjustments compress a ranking that looked like 3% to 9% into a much narrower band, and they reorder it.
