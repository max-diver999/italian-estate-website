Quick answer: building irregularities touch half or more of Italy's older resale stock, and the entire game is classification: deviations within the 2-6% Salva Casa tolerances cost €0 and need no procedure, partial difformità regularise for roughly €2,500-14,000 all-in, and total abuses (no-permit structures, added volumes) can never be regularised and must be priced at land-minus-demolition logic. A €300-1,500 conformity survey before the offer sorts any property into those bands. This guide maps the severity ladder, the discovery workflow, remedy costs, and the walk-away lines, with the 2024 reform's mechanics detailed in the [Salva Casa guide](/guides/salva-casa-decree-property-buyers/).

## What exactly is an abuso edilizio?

An abuso edilizio is any divergence between the built reality and the permitted project beyond legal tolerance, and Italian law grades the divergence into 4 bands whose consequences differ by orders of magnitude: within-tolerance deviations are lawful, partial difformità are fixable for fees, total difformità and no-permit structures face demolition orders that survive sales and decades.

| Band | Example | Legal position |
|---|---|---|
| Tolerance (2-6%) | wall 20 cm off plan on an 85 sqm flat | lawful, no procedure |
| Partial difformità | internal layout changed, window added, garage converted | sanabile (art. 36-bis) |
| Total difformità | extra floor, closed loggia adding volume | generally not sanabile |
| Assenza di titolo | structure built with no permit at all | demolition order, acquisition risk |

Two framing facts keep the subject proportionate:

- **Frequency**: practitioner estimates put urban resales carrying at least one deviation at 50% or more; the overwhelming majority sit in the first two bands.
- **Persistence**: abuses do not age out; a 1980s order is as enforceable as a 2020s one, which is why classification, not optimism, is the buyer's tool.

## Where do irregularities come from, and where do they cluster?

Italy's irregularity stock is a history lesson written in walls: 3 condono amnesties (1985, 1994, 2003) regularised millions of abuses against payment of oblazioni that funded whole budget laws, the boom decades built faster than comuni could file, and owners moved kitchens for 60 years without asking. Clustering follows that history by geography and asset type, and it prices in percent, not adjectives.

The risk map by market segment:

| Segment | Typical finding | Risk level |
|---|---|---|
| Post-1990 urban condominio flats | executional deviations inside tolerance | low |
| Pre-1975 city apartments | internal layout vs 1960s plans | low-medium, usually sanabile |
| Rural farmhouses and masserie | annexes, converted stalls, no comprehensive title | medium-high |
| Coastal villas of the condono decades | added volumes, verandas, whole floors | high |
| Anything in vincolo areas | works without Soprintendenza consent | high, own regime |

Puglia illustrates the pattern at both ends: Bari's post-war condominio stock verifies quickly and cleanly, while trulli, lamie and coastal villas around the Valle d'Itria carry decades of incremental annexes, cisterns turned bedrooms and pool surrounds built on a Sunday. Neither end is unbuyable; they simply demand different survey depth, and rural files justify upgrading the standard checks in the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-italy-property/) to a full conformity survey with the permit archive opened.

Condono arithmetic still shapes today's files: the 1985 amnesty drew roughly 4 million applications, 1994 and 2003 added millions more, and comuni are processing the tail of those queues to this day, so a seller's «condono presented» line can mean a title perfected 30 years ago or an application still pending with fees unpaid. The difference is contractual, not cosmetic: a pending condono transfers to the buyer as a pending risk, priced accordingly, while a perfected one is simply part of the stato legittimo. On coastal stock the 2003 amnesty matters doubly, because it excluded protected zones, which is precisely where the boom of that decade built: a veranda «condonata» inside a coastal band deserves a lawyer's reading of the actual comune decision, never the listing's one-word summary of it.

**Insider tip:** on rural stock, ask the seller one question before commissioning anything: «what does the accesso agli atti show?». A seller who has already pulled the comune's permit file and hands it over is selling a knowable property; a seller who has never looked is selling you the job of finding out, and the price should reflect who is doing that work.

## How do you discover irregularities before committing?

Discovery is a triangulation exercise with 3 documents and a tape measure: the cadastral planimetria, the comune's building-permit file, and the measured reality must all tell one story, and the professional who runs the comparison is a geometra at €300-1,500 depending on property complexity. The sequence belongs before the offer, because discovery order decides who pays.

The workflow, in the order that catches the most for the least:

1. **Planimetria vs reality**: the geometra measures rooms against the filed plan; mismatches surface 80% of everyday deviations in a 2-hour visit.
2. **Accesso agli atti**: the comune's file shows every permit, SCIA and condono ever filed; gaps between built volume and titled volume surface here.
3. **Title-deed declarations**: prior deeds' urbanistic declarations reveal what earlier sellers admitted, and sometimes what they omitted.
4. **Classification memo**: each finding graded into the 4 bands with a remedy cost, the document your [offer conditions](/guides/italy-property-offer-negotiation/) will reference.

The professional's role and fee structure, including when an architect or structural engineer replaces the geometra, are profiled in the [geometra guide](/guides/geometra-role-italy-property/); what matters here is sequencing. A conformity memo dated before the proposta turns every finding into the seller's problem to fix or discount; the same memo dated after acceptance turns findings into your renegotiation battle, fought from inside a binding contract.

Survey economics justify themselves on the first file: at €300-1,500 the conformity check costs 0.1-0.4% of a typical purchase, while the median finding it surfaces (an internal-layout difformità needing €3,000-8,000 of regularisation) reprices the deal by 10-20 times the fee, and the tail findings (unpermitted volume on 1 rural file in perhaps 5) save the entire purchase. Sequencing multiplies the return: the identical memo delivered before the proposta funds itself through the seller's concession, delivered after acceptance it merely budgets your own repair. Buyers who internalise that asymmetry stop treating the survey as a cost line at all: it is the cheapest option contract available in Italian real estate, and it is purchased on the only asset that ever matters in this game, information.

Auction buyers run a compressed version of the same triangulation on the court's perizia, which must describe irregularities and their sanabilità: the [auctions guide](/guides/italy-property-auctions-foreigners/) covers how those paragraphs price into bids on stock where no seller will ever fund the fix.

## What does each remedy cost after Salva Casa?

Remedy economics now split cleanly along the severity ladder: tolerance items cost €0 by definition, partial difformità regularise for €2,500-14,000 all-in (oblazione plus technician plus cadastral updates), and total abuses have no remedy price because no procedure exists, leaving demolition or indefinite risk as the only outcomes.

| Finding | Procedure | All-in cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Within 2-6% tolerance | none | €0 | immediate |
| Internal layout changes | CILA tardiva / art. 36-bis | €1,500-5,000 | 4-10 weeks |
| Façade/window changes, small conversions | art. 36-bis sanatoria | €3,000-14,000 | 1-3 months |
| Missing agibilità paperwork | SCA filing | €1,000-3,000 | weeks |
| Added volume, extra floor | none available | not fixable | n/a |
| No-permit structure | none (condoni closed 2003) | not fixable | n/a |

Three costing rules from practice:

- **Price the fix at 100-150% of the quote** when deducting from an offer: regularisations overrun on comune queries, and the margin is your patience premium.
- **Sequence matters for financing**: banks lend against the regularised state, so sanatoria-before-rogito keeps the mortgage simple; sanatoria-after requires the bank to accept the pending file, and many decline.
- **Bundle filings**: one art. 36-bis application covering all partial items costs materially less than serial fixes discovered one buyer at a time.

The reform's exact tolerance bands, the lightened conformity test and the mini-flat habitability rules sit in the [Salva Casa guide](/guides/salva-casa-decree-property-buyers/); the cost table above is what they translate to at the negotiation table.

## When should a buyer walk away?

Walk-away discipline reduces to one principle: never own a problem without a procedure, because a €5,000 sanatoria is a repair while an unfixable abuse is a permanent lien on value enforced at the comune's discretion. The four red lines below have survived every market cycle, and each has a documented failure mode behind it.

1. **Added volume on the title's silence**: an extra floor, a closed loggia grown into a room, a taverna that the permit file has never met. No current procedure regularises new volume outside the historic condoni, and demolition orders against 30-40 year old structures are routinely upheld.
2. **Protected-zone works**: structures in coastal bands, riverbeds and landslide red zones fail planning conformity by definition; the [heritage guide](/guides/heritage-restricted-property-italy/)'s vincoli operate the same way for listed contexts.
3. **Structural works without engineering titles**: seismic-rule violations, covered in the [seismic zones guide](/guides/seismic-zones-italy-property/), carry safety and insurance consequences no fee cures.
4. **A seller who resists the survey**: obstruction of the accesso agli atti or refusal of a conformity condition is information, and it is never good information.

Economic translation of the red lines: an unfixable structure prices at land value minus demolition cost, a formula that lands 40-60% below the asking prices such properties typically carry, and a seller who will not meet that arithmetic is not selling yet. Buyers who internalise this walk into perhaps 1 negotiation in 10 with a hard line and out of it with either a defensible asset or their capital intact, the exact asymmetry the [buyer mistakes guide](/guides/mistakes-foreign-buyers-italy/) documents from its failure-case side.

## How do irregularities interact with the deed and the notary?

The notary's role is declarations, not walls: Italian deeds must contain the seller's urbanistic declarations (titles, condoni, the permitted state), a deed with truthful declarations validly transfers even imperfect stock, and 0% of the notary's mandatory checks involve measuring a room. False declarations expose the seller to nullity actions and damages, so the buyer's real protection is verification plus contract, never the deed alone.

How the machinery distributes responsibility:

| Actor | Checks | Does not check |
|---|---|---|
| Notary | declarations present and formally coherent, title chain, cadastral identifiers | whether walls match plans |
| Bank's surveyor | conformity sufficient for the loan | items outside lending criteria |
| Geometra (yours) | measured reality vs titles vs cadastre | nothing, if actually instructed |
| Seller | owes truthful declarations | your diligence |

Holdback mechanics deserve their own numbers because they solve the commonest impasse: a seller who admits a sanabile defect but wants to close on schedule. The pattern that works leaves 5-10% of the price (€20,000-40,000 on typical stock) on the notary's dedicated account at the rogito, released against the comune's closure of the sanatoria file, with a longstop date after which the buyer may apply the funds to the regularisation directly. The seller closes on time, the buyer never funds the fix from goodwill, and the notary's escrow rules (the same deposito prezzo machinery used for mortgage cancellations) make the arrangement routine rather than exotic. Agencies rarely propose it because it delays their commission's final tranche; buyers should propose it themselves whenever a file closes faster than a comune does.

Contract craft completes the protection: a conformity clause in the proposta making regularised paper a condition of completion, seller warranties surviving the rogito for undeclared items, and, on sanabile stock, a holdback (5-10% of price at the notary) released when the comune's file closes. Each device is standard, each costs goodwill in proportion to the property's actual state, and a seller's resistance to them prices the risk more honestly than any listing text. The agibilità certificate, a separate document with its own gaps on older stock, completes this paper landscape and has [its own guide](/guides/agibilita-certificate-italy/).

Buyer scenarios close the loop on how much rigour each purchase needs. The urban flat buyer runs the standard triangulation and usually finds tolerance-band trivia: 1 week, €400, done. The masseria buyer budgets a full conformity survey with the permit archive opened, 3-4 weeks and €1,000-1,500, because rural files hide the expensive bands. The auction bidder gets the court's perizia free but no counterparty, so its abuse paragraphs set the bid ceiling directly.

Bought well, imperfect stock is often the value pocket of the Italian market: the discount for a fixable defect routinely exceeds the fix. Bought blind, the same stock is where foreign buyers' worst stories start, and the difference between the two outcomes is a survey that costs less than the flight that follows it.
