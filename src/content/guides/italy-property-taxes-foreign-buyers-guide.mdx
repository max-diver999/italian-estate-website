IMU on non-resident villas in Puglia and Tuscany commonly runs 1,800 to 3,200 euros annually on 450k to 650k euro tickets. Cedolare secca at 21% on first STR unit remains the default election for UK and US landlords; budget 26% when scaling to a second tourist property. Plusvalenza at 26% bites flips under five years; hold beyond five years for CGT-free exit unless primary residence exemption applies. Model net yield after Italian and home-country layers before offer, not after rogito.

**Insider tip:** Cadastral value often sits 35% to 50% below market price on legacy centro storico stock, which reduces registration tax on resale but does not reduce IMU base proportionally because IMU uses the same rendita with coefficient 160. Run both purchase and annual lines in the same spreadsheet.

| Tax | Position for a non-resident second home |
|---|---|
| Registration, from a private seller | 9% of cadastral value |
| Ipotecaria and catastale | €50 each |
| IMU | 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value |
| Long-let income | 21% under cedolare secca |
| Capital gains | Taxable if sold within 5 years |

- Every figure here is statutory; the comune sets the actual IMU rate for the address.
- A commercialista should confirm the position before any deposit is wired.
- Prima casa relief does not apply to a non-resident second home.
