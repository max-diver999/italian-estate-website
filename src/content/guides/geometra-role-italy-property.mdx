Quick answer: the geometra is the professional Italy's purchase system quietly assumes but never mandates: a licensed surveyor-technician who, for €300-1,500, measures whether the walls, the cadastre and the permit file agree, the single check that catches more expensive surprises than every other verification combined. The notary audits paper against paper; only the geometra audits paper against reality. This guide covers what the profession does, fees by task, the boundaries with architects and engineers, the filings you will meet (DOCFA, CILA, SCIA), and hiring one from abroad, inside the wider [due diligence](/guides/due-diligence-italy-property/) machinery.

## What does a geometra actually do?

The geometra is a regulated technical profession (qualifying exams, provincial Collegio registration, professional insurance) whose territory is surveying, cadastral management, modest building projects and works filings, priced for private clients between €150 for a filing and €1,500 for a complex survey. In purchase practice that means 4 services, and the first is the one this site never stops recommending.

| Service | What it involves | Typical fee |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-purchase conformity check | measure reality vs planimetria vs permit file | €300-1,500 |
| Cadastral filings (DOCFA) | updated plans, mergers, splits, category changes | €150-400 per filing |
| Works permits and direction | CILA, SCIA, end-of-works, modest project direction | €500-3,000 by scope |
| Certifications | APE energy certificates, agibilità attestations | €150-500 |

Profession-scale numbers explain why availability is never the problem: Italy's collegi count roughly 80,000 registered geometri, provincial registers from Lucca to Palermo list them publicly with contact details, and the profession's historic role as the countryside's all-in-one technician means even 2,000-inhabitant comuni usually have 1-2 members in practice. Fees stayed accessible for the same structural reason: the profession prices for private households, not corporations, with the standard purchase check at €300-500 essentially nationwide and remarkably little North-South spread in the quoted bands. For a foreign buyer the practical consequence is that the binding constraint is never finding a geometra: it is briefing the chosen one well and early enough in the purchase calendar, which costs nothing at all except a little foresight.

The profession's position in the transaction explains its value: Italy's closing system concentrates verification in the [notary](/guides/notaio-italy-property-role/), who audits title, declarations and formalities to a high standard, and in the bank's appraiser, who checks what lending requires. Neither measures rooms. The geometra is the only actor whose job is the physical truth of the asset, and the purchase system treats that truth as the buyer's private responsibility.

One habit, worth 2-3 weeks of calendar on every file, distinguishes buyers who extract full value from the profession: they send the geometra the listing before the viewing trip, not after it. Photographs and cadastral identifiers are enough for a desk pre-read that flags which of the 4 service lines the property will actually need, and the pre-read is usually free.

Numbers make the case briskly: the check costs 0.1-0.4% of a typical purchase, surfaces its findings within 1-2 weeks, and the modal finding on pre-1990 stock (a layout that no longer matches the filed plans) costs €1,500-5,000 to regularise when priced calmly before an offer, and multiples of that in delay and leverage when discovered by the bank's surveyor 3 weeks before a scheduled rogito.

## When is the geometra the right professional, and when not?

Competence boundaries are legally defined and practically simple: the geometra owns surveying, cadastre and modest civil works; the architect adds design authority and the heritage contexts where a Soprintendenza reads the drawings; the structural engineer owns calculations wherever structure or seismic performance is at stake. Purchases start with the geometra and escalate by property type.

| Situation | Right professional | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Standard apartment or house check | geometra | the core competence, best price |
| Zone 1-2 masonry, structural doubts | + structural engineer (€800-2,500) | the [seismic file](/guides/seismic-zones-italy-property/) needs calculations |
| Listed building, historic centre vincoli | + architect | [heritage](/guides/heritage-restricted-property-italy/) authorities expect design language |
| Renovation project, ordinary scope | geometra (+ engineer for structure) | permits, direction, filings |
| New build from developer | geometra for snagging | the [warranty](/guides/italy-new-build-warranty-defects/) verbale needs a professional list |

Language rarely blocks the escalation chain in practice: engineers and architects working heritage and coastal markets increasingly quote in English, and where they do not, the geometra habitually plays interpreter for the technical pair, one more argument for choosing that first professional partly on communication. A briefing exchanged in clear written English, answered in kind within 3 days, predicts the whole engagement's quality better than any directory credential.

Escalation is additive, not competitive: on a Lucca hills farmhouse with a cracked lintel and an annexe of uncertain paperwork, the working team is a geometra running the file plus an engineer pricing the structure, €1,500-3,000 combined, and the two documents together convert a romantic ruin into a priced project. Italian practice pairs these professions daily; the buyer's only job is to start the chain early enough that its findings land before the offer binds.

Buyer scenarios calibrate the escalation budget: the apartment buyer in Bologna needs the €400 geometra check and nothing else in 9 cases of 10; the farmhouse buyer near Lucca should assume geometra plus engineer from the first viewing and budget €1,500-3,000 for the pair; the buyer of anything inside a historic-centre vincolo adds the architect's €500-1,500 feasibility opinion before falling in love, because heritage consent shapes what any renovation may even attempt.

**Insider tip:** brief the geometra with questions, not tasks. «Is the annexe legal, what would regularisation cost, and would you buy this file at this price?» produces professional judgement; «do a conformity check» produces a template. The profession answers what it is asked, and the second question costs the same €500 as the first.

## What are the filings a buyer will meet (DOCFA, CILA, SCIA)?

Three acronyms are most of what the geometra files on your behalf: DOCFA updates the cadastre's plans and data for €150-400, CILA notifies light renovation works, and SCIA certifies the start of heavier ones. Knowing the trio turns permit conversations into information, because each filing has a cost band and a specific failure mode when skipped.

| Filing | What it is for | Cost via geometra | Skipped = |
|---|---|---|---|
| DOCFA | cadastral plan/data updates after changes | €150-400 | stale planimetria, stalled sales |
| CILA | light works notification (layouts, systems) | €300-800 incl. drafting | partial difformità to fix later |
| SCIA | certified start for heavier works | €500-1,500 | abuso territory, per the [severity ladder](/guides/abusi-edilizi-buying-property-italy/) |
| SCA (agibilità) | certified habitability after works | €300-800 | the gaps the [agibilità guide](/guides/agibilita-certificate-italy/) maps |

Two workflow rules keep the filings honest across a purchase-plus-renovation arc:

1. **Buy clean, then file forward**: the seller regularises the past (their DOCFA, their sanatorias); your geometra files the future (your CILA, your end-of-works, your updated plans). Mixing the two ledgers is how buyers pay for other people's history.
2. **Every works project ends with paper**: the renovation is not finished at the last coat of paint but at the end-of-works filing plus DOCFA plus, where relevant, the SCA; the [renovation guide](/guides/italy-property-renovation-costs-guide/) budgets these closing filings at €800-2,000 and treats them as part of the works, because the next sale will.

Filing discipline has a measurable resale payoff: a property whose paper trail closes cleanly (CILA, end-of-works, DOCFA within 30 days of completion, SCA where due) sells against its documents in days, while the identical renovation with open filings resurfaces at the next sale as a €1,500-5,000 regularisation plus weeks of delay, priced against the then-seller at the worst possible moment. Italian practitioners put a large share of stalled closings down to exactly this class of gap, stale planimetrie above all, and the fix costs 10 times less when filed contemporaneously than when reconstructed years later from invoices and memory. The geometra's closing filings are, in effect, prepaid insurance on the next transaction, purchased at €800-2,000 for a whole renovation's paper and redeemed at the following rogito.

Salva Casa moved the goalposts kindly here: the 2024 tolerances mean many historic micro-deviations no longer need any filing at all, and the geometra's classification memo, tolerance versus sanabile versus abuse, is now the document that decides which acronym, if any, a finding triggers, mechanics the [Salva Casa guide](/guides/salva-casa-decree-property-buyers/) details band by band.

## What does the geometra cost across a full ownership cycle?

Cycle costing puts the profession's fees in proportion: a foreign owner who buys, renovates modestly, holds 10 years (the median foreign holding period runs 8-12 years) and sells will typically spend €2,500-5,500 on geometra services across the entire arc, less than 1.5% of a €300,000 asset's value, spread over 5 distinct engagements that each protect a materially larger number.

| Stage | Engagement | Fee band |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase | conformity check + classification memo | €300-1,500 |
| Renovation | CILA/SCIA + direction + closing filings | €1,300-3,500 |
| Ownership | occasional DOCFA, certifications | €150-500 as needed |
| Sale | pre-listing conformity refresh, APE | €300-600 |

Comparative context makes the bands legible for buyers from survey-heavy markets: a UK homebuyer's RICS survey runs 400-1,500 pounds, roughly 0.2-0.5% of price, for a report that stops at condition, while the Italian geometra's memo at similar money covers condition plus legal conformity plus the cadastral position, the three layers Italian transactions actually turn on.

Two budgeting habits keep the cycle cheap. First, bundle: the professional who ran your purchase check typically prices renovation filings 15-20% lower, because the measuring is already done. Second, never defer the closing filings: every €300 DOCFA skipped at works' end returns as a €1,500 regularisation at sale, with a deadline attached, the arithmetic the resale-payoff numbers above already priced.

## How do you hire and brief a geometra from abroad?

Hiring is a solved remote workflow: the provincial Collegio dei Geometri publishes its register (Lucca's alone lists hundreds of members), agents volunteer names freely, video calls are routine, and the entire engagement, briefing, site visit, written memo, runs comfortably from another country in 1-2 weeks for the standard purchase check.

The remote engagement sequence that produces good work:

1. **Source 2-3 candidates**: the Collegio register plus one agent recommendation, minus anyone the selling side proposed for the conformity role.
2. **Send a complete brief**: cadastral identifiers (foglio, particella, subalterno from the listing or visura), the advert, your specific doubts, and your calendar.
3. **Ask for a written quote** naming the checks (measurement, permit file access, classification memo) and the deliverable format.
4. **Fix the deliverable as a document**: photographs, measured deviations, band classification, regularisation estimates; €100-200 more than a verbal report and worth every euro at negotiation.
5. **Schedule against the offer**, not the trip: findings must land while conditions can still absorb them, the sequencing the [viewing-trip guide](/guides/italy-property-viewing-trip-checklist/) builds its day 3 around.

Independence deserves its own line because the failure is common and quiet: the geometra who managed the seller's property for 15 years knows it best and audits it worst, since a conformity memo criticising their own DOCFA filings from 2011 is simply not a document any professional ever writes. The purchase-stage rule is absolute (your check, your professional, your €400), while post-purchase, hiring the building's historic technician for future works is often the efficient choice precisely because of that accumulated knowledge. The same conflict logic runs through the whole professional cast of an Italian purchase, and the buyer who pays for 3-4 hours of genuinely independent technical time is buying the only commodity the transaction cannot generate internally: an opinion with 0% stake in whether the closing happens.

Deliverable quality separates the €400 that protects from the €400 that decorates: a purchase memo worth the fee contains measured floor plans against the filed planimetria, photographs keyed to findings, each deviation classified into the tolerance/sanabile/abuse bands with an estimated regularisation cost, and a one-page conclusion a notary or negotiator can act on. Verbal reassurance («tutto a posto, more or less») delivers 0% of that value, and it is exactly what buyers receive when they do not specify the deliverable. The specification costs one sentence in the engagement email and perhaps €100-200 on the invoice, and every subsequent actor in the deal, your lawyer, the bank's surveyor, the seller's agent facing your documented price reduction, then works from that one memo rather than from anyone's impressions or anyone's optimism.

Fee benchmarks summarised for budgeting: €300-500 apartment check, €500-1,000 house with land, €1,000-1,500 rural complex, €150-400 per DOCFA, €800-2,000 for a renovation's closing filings, and €0 for the first video call in which you decide whether this is the professional you trust with the measuring. Against a market where the median dispute this profession prevents runs five figures, the geometra remains the best-priced instrument in the [foreign buyer's toolkit](/guides/buy-property-italy-foreigner/).
