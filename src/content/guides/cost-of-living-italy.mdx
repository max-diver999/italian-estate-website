Ask what Italy costs and you get a national average, which is the least useful number available. The spread between a flat in Milan and a house in inland Molise is wider than the average is informative, and the item that moves most between them is the one nobody can avoid: housing. Everything else on the list moves within a band you can plan around.

**Quick answer:** a single person outside the big northern cities lives on roughly €1,300 to €1,900 a month including rent; in Milan the same life runs €2,200 to €3,000. Housing accounts for almost the entire spread. Owning rather than renting adds IMU at a statutory 0.86% of cadastral value, TARI, condominio fees and maintenance. To be allowed to stay on an Elective Residence Visa you need €31,160 a year in passive income, which is a legal threshold rather than a cost of living.

## What does it actually cost to live in Italy each month?

Two households in the same country can differ by a factor of two, and almost all of that difference is rent or mortgage. Strip housing out and Italian monthly spending is remarkably flat from Turin to Lecce: food, phone, transport and insurance move by tens of euros, not hundreds.

| Line | Single, mid-size city | Single, Milan | Couple, mid-size city |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent, one or two bedrooms | €550 to €800 | €1,300 to €1,900 | €650 to €950 |
| Utilities and internet | €130 to €220 | €160 to €250 | €170 to €280 |
| Food, home and eating out | €300 to €450 | €400 to €600 | €500 to €750 |
| Transport | €35 to €90 | €39 season ticket | €70 to €180 |
| Health cover | €0 to €120 | €0 to €120 | €0 to €240 |
| Everything else | €150 to €300 | €250 to €450 | €250 to €500 |
| **Monthly total** | **€1,300 to €1,900** | **€2,200 to €3,000** | **€1,750 to €2,600** |

Read the bands rather than the midpoints. The low end of each is a person who rents outside the historic centre, cooks most nights and does not run a car. The high end is a person who does the opposite, and the gap between those two lives is larger than the gap between two Italian cities.

A couple costs far less than two singles. Rent, internet, the standing charges on utilities and the television licence do not double, so the second adult typically adds €450 to €700 rather than a second full budget.

## Which Italian cities are cheapest and most expensive?

Milan sits alone at the top, priced like a northern European capital rather than an Italian one, and every other city is a long way below it. At the other end, the cheapest places to live are not the famous ones: they are inland towns in the south and the Apennine centre where the housing stock is old, plentiful and unloved.

| City or area | Rent, one bedroom | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Milan | €1,300 to €1,900 | Alone at the top |
| Florence | €900 to €1,300 | Pushed up by short-let demand on the same stock |
| Rome | €800 to €1,200 | Wide internal spread by quarter |
| Bologna | €750 to €1,100 | Student demand keeps the floor high |
| Turin | €500 to €800 | The value outlier among big northern cities |
| Naples | €500 to €800 | Central, cheap, and rising |
| Lecce, Perugia, Ancona | €400 to €650 | Small cities, full services |
| Inland Molise, Basilicata, Calabria | €300 to €500 | Cheapest, and furthest from an airport |

Turin is the answer most people arrive at by elimination. It has the museums, the transport, the airport and the hospitals of a major northern city at close to half Milan's housing cost, and it is the one comparison that changes minds when it is put in front of someone who assumed the north was uniformly expensive.

Florence deserves a warning. Its rents behave like a tourist market rather than a residential one, because the same apartments compete with short lets, and the UNESCO core has restricted new short-term registrations since May 2025. That squeezes supply in both directions at once.

## How does Italy compare with the United States?

Some categories move sharply in Italy's favour and others sharply against, and which side you land on depends less on the country than on how you personally spend. The rule of thumb: Italy is cheaper for people, dearer for things.

| Category | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | Much cheaper outside Milan | Purchase prices per square metre and rents both sit far below comparable US metros |
| Healthcare | Much cheaper | Public system funded from taxation, private cover priced as a top-up rather than as primary insurance |
| Public transport | Much cheaper | Season tickets in the tens of euros, intercity rail competitive with driving |
| Eating and drinking out | Cheaper | Coffee, wine and a neighbourhood meal are not luxury purchases |
| Fresh food | Cheaper | Short supply chains, strong local markets |
| Electricity | Dearer | Among the higher household rates in Europe |
| Fuel and cars | Much dearer | Fuel duty, tolls, purchase taxes, insurance |
| Electronics and imported goods | Dearer | Smaller market, higher VAT |
| Domestic flights | Dearer per mile | Fewer routes, less competition |

Americans who were spending heavily on rent and health insurance see the largest fall. Americans who drive everywhere, buy a lot of imported goods and heat a large house see the smallest, and occasionally see none at all.

One line that catches people out: electricity. A stone farmhouse with electric heating and no insulation can produce a winter bill that erases the housing saving on its own. The building's energy class is a running-cost fact, not a paperwork detail, and our guide on [energy class and APE certificates](/guides/energy-class-ape-italy-property/) covers how to read it before you commit.

## What changes for an American specifically?

Two obligations follow the passport rather than the address, and neither disappears when you move. The United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live, so an American in Italy files in both countries and relies on the treaty and the foreign tax credit to avoid paying twice. Budget for a preparer who handles both sides.

The second is banking. US reporting rules make some Italian banks reluctant to open accounts for American clients, and the ones that do may take longer and ask for more. Start the account conversation before you need it, not in the week the notary wants funds.

Beyond that, the differences are practical rather than financial:

- Prescription medication is cheap, but the specific brand you use may not exist and the equivalent needs a local prescription
- Credit scores do not transfer, so a mortgage as a non-resident is assessed on income and deposit rather than history
- Rentals are usually let unfurnished to a standard that includes no kitchen at all, which is a real one-off cost
- Deposits and agency fees on a lease commonly run to three or four months of rent combined

## What does owning rather than renting add to the monthly figure?

Ownership swaps a predictable monthly rent for a set of annual bills that arrive in lumps, and the mistake is to compare a mortgage payment against a rent and stop there. On a home that is not your registered primary residence, four recurring items sit on top.

| Item | What it is | Rough scale |
|---|---|---|
| IMU | Municipal property tax on non-primary residences | Statutory base rate 0.86% of cadastral value, which each comune may reduce to zero or raise to 1.06% |
| TARI | Waste collection, set by the comune | Varies with floor area and occupancy, billed annually or in instalments |
| Condominio | Shared building costs where the building has them | Lift, stairwell, roof, cleaning, sometimes heating |
| Maintenance and insurance | Roof, boiler, garden, cover | Older and detached means higher |

Two things matter about IMU. It is calculated on the cadastral value rather than what you paid, and cadastral values are usually far below market price, so the headline percentage overstates the bill. And it applies to second homes: if the property is your registered primary residence and not in a luxury cadastral category, the picture changes. Our [IMU guide](/guides/imu-property-tax-italy/) has the full rule and the comune variation, and [property management costs](/guides/italy-property-management-costs/) covers condominio fees in detail.

Divide the annual total by twelve before comparing with rent. A €400,000 second home carrying IMU, TARI, condominio and a maintenance reserve will typically add a few hundred euros a month to the mortgage line, and a rural house with land adds more than a city flat does.

## How much income do you need to be allowed to stay?

Permission and affordability are different tests, and the legal one is usually the higher of the two. For the Elective Residence Visa, which is the route most non-EU retirees and people living on capital take, the published floor is €31,160 a year in passive income for a single applicant, and consulates in practice look for materially more before approving a file.

That number sits above what living in much of southern and inland Italy actually costs, which produces the situation people find counterintuitive: you can comfortably afford a town in Basilicata on an income that will not get you the visa to live there. The threshold is a test of whether you will be a burden on the state, not a cost-of-living calculation, and it must be passive. Remote work income does not count for this route and presenting it is the most common reason files are refused.

Different routes apply different tests. The [elective residence guide](/guides/italy-elective-residence-visa-property/) covers the passive income evidence, and the [digital nomad guide](/guides/digital-nomad-italy-property-guide/) covers the route for people who still work.

## How does healthcare cost work once you live there?

Registration with the national health service is the step that changes the number, and it is available once you hold a residence permit and register with the comune. Before that, the visa itself requires private cover, so the first months carry an insurance premium that later becomes optional.

Once enrolled, non-EU residents who are not working generally pay an annual voluntary contribution rather than a monthly premium, and that contribution buys the same access as any resident: a family doctor, hospital treatment, and prescriptions at controlled prices. Many people keep a modest private policy alongside it to shorten waiting times for scans and specialist appointments rather than to replace the public system.

The practical budget effect is that healthcare moves from being one of the largest lines in an American household budget to one of the smallest. It is the single biggest reason US arrivals report a fall in total spending even when they move to an expensive city.

## What do people underestimate?

Five items account for most of the gap between a planned budget and a real one, and none of them are the ones that get discussed.

- **Heating an old house.** The romance of a stone farmhouse and its January bill are the same building. Ask for twelve months of actual bills before you offer.
- **The car you said you would not need.** In a small town it is not optional, and fuel, insurance, tolls and the annual roadworthiness test add up quickly.
- **Getting home.** Flights to the US or UK from a regional Italian airport, several times a year, at short notice, are a real annual line.
- **Furnishing.** Unfurnished in Italy often means no kitchen, no light fittings and no wardrobes.
- **Professional fees in year one.** Codice fiscale, a commercialista, translations, a lawyer for the purchase, and the notary. These are one-off but they land together.

The counterweight is that the recurring lines are genuinely lower and stay lower. Households that survive the first year without a nasty surprise usually report that year two and three cost less than they expected rather than more.

## Which of these numbers are fixed and which drift?

Not every figure on this page has the same shelf life, and treating them as equally solid is how a budget goes wrong in month four. Three tiers are worth separating.

**Set in law and stable for years.** IMU has a statutory base rate of 0.86% of cadastral value under the 2020 budget law, with each comune free to reduce it to 0% or raise it to 1.06%. VAT sits at 22% on most goods. Cedolare secca on short lets runs at 21% on the first residential unit a landlord lets in a tax year and 26% from the second. These move only when parliament moves them, which is roughly once every 4 years and always announced.

**Set annually and worth rechecking.** The Elective Residence income floor, TARI rates in your comune, and the voluntary health service contribution are all reset on a yearly cycle. Check them for the year you apply rather than the year you research, because a file assembled against 2025 numbers and submitted in 2026 gets sent back.

**Genuinely volatile.** Rents in Florence and Milan, electricity, and anything priced against tourism. Florence has restricted new short-term rental registrations in its UNESCO core since May 2025, which pushed some stock back into long lets and pulled rents in two directions at once over 18 months. Electricity has been the least predictable household line in Italy for several years running.

The practical rule: build the budget on the stable tier, verify the annual tier for your application year, and hold a reserve of 3 to 6 months against the volatile tier. Households that plan a 12 month runway and hold nothing back are the ones who leave in year two.

**Pros of the Italian cost base.** Healthcare, public transport, fresh food and eating out are all materially cheaper than a comparable US metro, and a purchase at €2,000 to €3,000 per m² outside the top cities buys space that would be unattainable at home.

**Cons.** Electricity, fuel, cars and imported goods are dearer; an old house with no insulation can consume the entire housing saving through the winter; and salaries, if you intend to earn locally, are far below US levels for the same work.

## Who this is for, and which budget applies to you

Three profiles account for most people running these numbers, and the same national figures mean different things to each.

| Buyer profile | Where the budget bites | What to plan for |
|---|---|---|
| Retired, living on a pension | The visa income floor sits above the cost of living in the south, so permission is the binding constraint, not affordability | Prove passive income comfortably above €31,160, then choose the region freely |
| Still working, remote, for a foreign employer | Milan and Rome pricing, plus the 183 day tax residency line | Model the flat-tax regime with a commercialista before, not after, the move |
| Buying a second home, not relocating | Only the ownership column matters: IMU, TARI, condominio, maintenance | Twelve months of real utility bills from the seller before you offer |

A fourth case is worth naming because it goes wrong most often: the person who runs the numbers for a holiday town in August. Coastal Puglia, the lakes and the Amalfi coast price and behave differently outside the season, restaurants close, and the year-round reality is a different place from the one you visited.

### Red flags and what to check before committing

- **No heating bills offered.** Ask for 12 months of actual utility bills. A seller who will not produce them is telling you something.
- **A budget built on the midpoint.** Model the top of each band, not the middle. The bands on this page are wide because the underlying reality is.
- **Rent quoted without the deposit stack.** Deposit plus agency fee commonly runs to 3 or 4 months of rent, paid before you move in.
- **Cadastral value assumed to equal price.** IMU is calculated on cadastral value, and a seller quoting IMU from an old, unrevised cadastral entry is quoting a number that may change.
- **A car assumed to be optional.** In towns under roughly 30,000 people it is not, and it is a four-figure annual line.
