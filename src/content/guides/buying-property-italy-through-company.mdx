Quick answer: for a single home or holiday flat, buying in your own name beats an Italian SRL in roughly nine cases out of ten. The company loses the prezzo-valore purchase base, loses cedolare secca's 21% flat rate, loses the five-year capital gains exemption, and adds 2,500-5,000 euros of running cost a year. A structure earns its keep only for professional letting at three or more units, renovation-and-resale projects, or multi-investor deals. This guide runs the numbers on both paths using the same Milan apartment, then maps the four scenarios where incorporation genuinely wins. Eligibility basics for foreign buyers sit in the [buy property in Italy as a foreigner](/guides/buy-property-italy-foreigner/) hub; rate mechanics live in the [registration tax guide](/guides/italy-registration-tax-property/).

## Should you buy Italian property through a company?

For a home you will use or let casually the answer is no: on a 450,000 euro Milan apartment the SRL route costs about €24,600 more at purchase, then €8,000-9,000 extra every year against a cedolare secca landlord. The calculus flips only where the activity is genuinely a business, which Italian law itself presumes from the third short-let unit.

The decision splits into three moments, and the company loses at each one unless business-scale facts change the inputs:

1. **Purchase**: individuals tax the cadastral value; companies tax the price.
2. **Holding**: individuals elect a 21% flat rate on rent; companies pay 24% IRES on a base that ignores most residential costs.
3. **Exit**: individuals sell tax-free after five years; companies never do.

Each is quantified below with the same worked asset so the totals are comparable, not cherry-picked.

## How much more purchase tax does a company pay?

An individual buying resale residential property applies 9% registration tax to the cadastral value under the prezzo-valore rule; a company applies the same 9% to the actual price, because the rule is reserved for private buyers by the 2006 law that created it. On typical stock, where cadastral values trail market prices by 40-60%, this single difference roughly doubles the purchase-tax bill.

| Input | Individual buyer | SRL buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Milan apartment price | €450,000 | €450,000 |
| Cadastral rent (rendita) | €1,400 | €1,400 |
| Taxable base | €1,400 × 1.05 × 120 = €176,400 | €450,000 (price) |
| Registration tax 9% | €15,876 | €40,500 |
| Fixed taxes | €100 | €100 |
| **Difference** | — | **+€24,524** |

Prezzo-valore was written into the 2006 Finance Act as a consumer measure, and its perimeter has never widened: the buyer must be a natural person acting outside business activity, the property residential, and the request stated in the deed. A company signing the same rogito for the same 450,000 euro apartment is taxed on price, 40,500 euros against 15,876, and the 24,600 euro difference is not planning failure but statutory design. Cadastral values were last revalued generally in the 1990s, which is why they sit at 40-60% of market prices in most cities and even lower in appreciating districts. Every euro of market growth since then widens the company's disadvantage at purchase, because the individual's base stands still while the company's base is the negotiated price itself.

Buying new from a developer is the one neutral case:

- Both individual and SRL pay 10% VAT on the invoiced price (22% for luxury categories A/1, A/8, A/9), plus 600 euros of fixed taxes.
- A company targeting new stock therefore gives up nothing at this stage; the gap reopens only on resale purchases.

The full rate matrix, including the 2% prima casa track a company can never access, is in the [registration tax guide](/guides/italy-registration-tax-property/).

**Insider tip:** ask the notary to quote both computations on your actual visura before choosing the vehicle. On high-rendita city-centre stock the cadastral base can reach 70% of price and shrink the gap; on rural stock with a 500 euro rendita the company's extra tax alone can exceed a year of rent.

## How is rental income taxed: company vs individual?

The headline rates look close, 24% against 21%, but the bases are not. A private landlord's cedolare secca applies to gross rent with nothing deductible; an SRL letting residential units held as beni patrimonio is taxed on broadly the higher of cadastral income or rent less documented maintenance capped at 15%, with ordinary running costs, IMU and depreciation on the unit non-deductible under article 90 of the tax code.

Worked on 30,000 euros of annual rent from that Milan apartment:

| Line | Individual (cedolare 21%) | SRL |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable base | €30,000 | €25,500 (rent −15% cap) |
| Income tax | €6,300 | €6,120 (IRES 24%) |
| IRAP (3.9%, most regions) | — | ~€950 |
| Accounting and filings | €0 | €2,500-4,000 |
| Extraction as dividend (26%) | — | ~€4,700 on distributed net |
| **Annual all-in** | **€6,300** | **€14,000-15,500** |

Article 90 of the Italian tax code is the clause that breaks naive SRL spreadsheets: for residential property held as beni patrimonio, deductions for running costs are expressly denied, and the taxable base is the higher of the cadastral income or the rent reduced by documented maintenance capped at 15%. Condominium fees, management, insurance, IMU and depreciation all fall outside the computation, so a company collecting 30,000 euros of rent is taxed on at least 25,500 whatever it actually spent. The private landlord's cedolare secca taxes 100% of gross at 21%, which looks worse on the base and better on everything else: no IRAP, no 2,500-4,000 euros of accounting, and no second layer of tax to reach the owner's pocket. On identical rent the all-in gap runs 8,000-9,000 euros a year in the individual's favour.

The dividend line is the one investors forget: profit trapped in the company is not spendable, and taking it out costs 26% for individual shareholders. Where letting is short-term, remember the personal regime's own limits, 21% on the first unit and 26% on a second, detailed in the [cedolare secca guide](/guides/cedolare-secca-italy-rental-property/); from the third unit the law presumes a business for individuals too, which is exactly where the SRL comparison turns competitive.

## What happens to capital gains when the company sells?

A private seller's capital gain on Italian property becomes tax-free once five years have passed since purchase, main homes and inherited property even earlier. A company enjoys no holding-period relief at any point: the gain is business income at 24% IRES in year two or year twenty, and distributing the proceeds adds 26% dividend withholding for individual shareholders.

Take a 100,000 euro gain on the Milan apartment sold in year seven:

1. Individual seller: 0 euros of Italian tax under the five-year rule (mechanics and the exceptions, including the 2024 superbonus clawback, are in the [capital gains guide](/guides/italy-capital-gains-tax-property/)).
2. SRL: 24,000 euros IRES inside the company.
3. Shareholder extraction: roughly 19,800 euros more on the distributed balance.
4. Combined leakage: about 43% of a gain the individual kept whole.

For a develop-and-sell operation the same rule reads differently: the trader was never going to hold five years, deducts renovation costs the private seller cannot, and recovers VAT on works, so business treatment is the point rather than the penalty.

Nuda proprietà and usufruct splits change exit maths for private holders as well; that route has [its own guide](/guides/nuda-proprieta-bare-ownership-italy/).

## What does an Italian SRL cost to set up and run?

Incorporating an Italian SRL costs €2,000-3,500, and the process is formal by design: a notarial deed, Companies Register filing, chamber of commerce enrolment and a certified electronic address are all mandatory, while the legal minimum capital of 1 euro is commercially useless for property. The recurring bill is the real cost, and it requires budgeting 2,500-5,000 euros every single year.

| Item | One-off | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Notarial incorporation deed | €1,200-1,800 | — |
| Registration, stamp, chamber enrolment | €500-700 | — |
| Chamber of commerce fee | — | €120-200 |
| Accountant (books, VAT, corporate return) | — | €2,000-4,000 |
| Registered office / PEC / digital signature | €150-300 | €300-600 |
| Corporate bank account | — | €150-400 |

Three items on that table are legally unavoidable rather than optional service purchases:

- The notarial deed of incorporation and Companies Register filing.
- Double-entry books and an annual corporate tax return, even for one flat and one tenant.
- A certified electronic address (PEC) and registered office maintained for the company's whole life.

Over a ten-year hold the structure alone consumes 25,000-50,000 euros before a single euro of tax difference. Financing does not rescue the maths either: Italian banks treat single-asset property SRLs as commercial borrowers, with lower loan-to-value and pricing above the personal non-resident terms covered in the [mortgage guide](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-italy/).

## When does buying through a company make sense?

Incorporation is worth its €2,500-5,000 annual cost in four recurring situations, and the common thread is scale or trade rather than passive holding: 3+ short-let units, develop-and-sell projects, multi-investor purchases, and genuinely commercial use. If your plan matches none of the four, the default is personal ownership, refined if needed with the options in the [new-resident tax guide](/guides/italy-flat-tax-regime-new-residents/).

| Scenario | Why the SRL wins |
|---|---|
| 3+ short-let units run as a business | Business treatment is mandatory anyway from unit three; the SRL adds limited liability, staff contracts and VAT recovery on refits |
| Buy-renovate-sell | Renovation costs and 10% VAT on works become deductible/recoverable; exit tax was unavoidable inside 5 years regardless |
| Multiple investors | Shares split, transfer and inherit more cleanly than deeded co-ownership quotas; shareholder agreements beat comunione rules |
| Genuine commercial use | Offices, retail, agriturismo with services: instrumental property restores cost deductibility the residential regime denies |

A fifth case, inheritance planning through a family holding, works in specific estates but is an estate-planning decision first; the ownership-transfer rules that drive it are covered in the [inheritance guide](/guides/italy-inheritance-law-property-foreigners/).

Professional short-let operators should also weigh the operational rulebook, CIN codes and the third-unit business threshold, in the [short-term rental rules guide](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-italy/) before choosing a vehicle.

## Foreign company or Italian SRL?

Holding Italian property through an existing US LLC or UK Ltd is legal under the same reciprocity conditions that govern individuals, but it rarely improves the position: the 9%-on-price purchase tax still applies, Italy still taxes 100% of the rent and the gain at source, and the wrapper typically adds 2,000-4,000 euros of duplicate annual filings. Three practical frictions decide most cases:

1. **Tax code and banking.** The foreign entity needs an Italian codice fiscale, and opening a non-resident corporate account for rogito funds is slower than for individuals, a real constraint given the payment-traceability rules in the [money transfer guide](/guides/transferring-money-to-italy-property/).
2. **Permanent establishment risk.** Actively managed lettings can drag the foreign company into Italian corporate filings anyway, recreating the SRL's costs without its clarity.
3. **Home-country interaction.** US owners face LLC-vs-treaty characterisation questions; UK owners meet ATED-style anti-enveloping history at home. Cross-border advice at 3,000-6,000 euros tends to open, not close, the cost gap.

Where a corporate layer is justified, an Italian SRL owned by the foreign entity is usually cleaner than direct foreign ownership: Italian counterparties, banks and the Companies Register all know what an SRL is.

## What is the società di comodo trap?

Italy taxes «comfort companies» on deemed income when a company holds assets without real activity: for residential property the presumed minimum revenue runs at roughly 6% of the asset value, imputed even if the flat sits empty or is used rent-free by the shareholder. A single-apartment SRL created to hold the family holiday home is the textbook fail case.

Consequences stack in three layers:

1. Minimum taxable income assessed at IRES 24% regardless of actual rent.
2. VAT credits blocked from refund and, after repeated failing years, lost.
3. Higher audit salience: the regime exists precisely to flag personal-use wrappers.

Operativity is tested arithmetically, not by intention: presumed revenue for residential assets runs at roughly 6% of their value, so an SRL holding a 400,000 euro apartment must show about 24,000 euros of receipts or be taxed as though it had earned a deemed minimum income anyway. A shareholder using the flat rent-free fails automatically, and below-market rent to a related party fails on assessment. The combined arithmetic is punitive: deemed income taxed at 24% IRES on money never received, VAT refund rights suspended, and, for gains, the same no-exemption exit as every company, which took roughly 43% of the worked example's 100,000 euro profit once dividends came out. The regime was drafted in 1994 precisely against single-asset wrappers, and forty years of case law have kept personal-use structures its main casualty.

Escape routes, market-rate letting to third parties, genuine trading activity, or interpello rulings on objective circumstances, all push the company toward behaving like the business it claimed to be. If the honest plan is personal enjoyment plus occasional letting, the personal regimes were designed for exactly that, and the wider due-diligence habits in the [buyer mistakes guide](/guides/mistakes-foreign-buyers-italy/) matter more than any wrapper.

**Insider tip:** before incorporating, ask your commercialista to run the operativity test on your projected rent against the actual purchase price. If projected rent is below roughly 6% of asset value, the shell-company regime will tax you on income you never earned, a worse outcome than either honest alternative.

## Which structure should you choose? The checklist

Choosing the vehicle is a 30-minute numbers exercise, not a philosophy: the six steps below use your actual price, rendita and rent, each is quantified in a section above, and step three alone decides the answer for most buyers. Run them in order before any notary appointment, with the visura catastale and rent projection in hand.

1. Compute both purchase-tax bases from the visura catastale: price × 9% versus rendita × 1.05 × 120 × 9%.
2. Project annual rent and apply both regimes: cedolare secca at 21% against IRES 24% on the article-90 base plus IRAP plus 2,500-5,000 euros of compliance.
3. Price the exit at year five and year ten under both rules, including 26% dividend extraction.
4. Test the società di comodo threshold: projected rent versus ~6% of asset value.
5. Count units and intent: three or more short lets, or a renovate-and-sell plan, are the genuine green lights.
6. If the company still wins, choose Italian SRL over a foreign wrapper unless home-country tax advice says otherwise.

For most readers the checklist ends at step three with a personal purchase and a better use for 30,000 euros of structure costs: the [full tax hub](/guides/italy-property-taxes-foreign-buyers-guide/) maps what the personal route owes instead.
