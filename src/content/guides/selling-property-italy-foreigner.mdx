Quick answer: selling Italian property as a foreigner is procedurally identical to selling as an Italian, and the whole game is preparation: a complete document file cuts months off the 5-7 month national average time-to-sale, agency costs run 2-4% plus VAT, and your tax exposure is usually zero after five years of ownership, except where the 2024 superbonus rule extends the clock to ten. This guide covers the document file, mandates and pricing, the deal sequence from offer to rogito, selling remotely by power of attorney, and the plusvalenza arithmetic, whose deeper mechanics live in the [capital gains guide](/guides/italy-capital-gains-tax-property/).

## Can a foreigner sell Italian property without restrictions?

Ownership settles the question: whoever validly owns Italian real estate can sell it, resident or not, EU or not, with 0 exit taxes, 0 permissions and no non-resident surcharge anywhere in the process. The seller's real obligations are the universal ones that bind every Italian seller equally: document completeness, payment traceability on 100% of the price, and tax on gains where the holding is short.

What a foreign seller must actually hold ready:

1. **Codice fiscale**: the same tax code from your purchase; replace a lost certificate before anything else.
2. **Capacity paperwork**: passports, and marital-regime evidence where the purchase deed recorded comunione dei beni.
3. **The property file**: assembled below; 90% of foreign-seller delays live here.
4. **A receiving account**: Italian preferred; the traceability rules from the [money transfer guide](/guides/transferring-money-to-italy-property/) apply to money leaving, too.

| Concern | Reality |
|---|---|
| Non-resident seller tax | none exists |
| Currency controls on proceeds | none; AML documentation only |
| Selling while abroad | routine via procura speciale |
| Buyer nationality limits | none from your side |

## Which documents decide whether your sale closes fast?

Documents are the sell-side battlefield: Italian sales average 5-7 months nationally, and the spread between fast and slow closings is mostly the state of the seller's file when the first offer lands. Ten items decide it, and eight of them can be fixed before listing for under €2,000 total.

| Document | Why it blocks without it | Fix cost / time |
|---|---|---|
| Atto di provenienza (title deed) | notary reconstructs your ownership from it | copy from any notary archive, €50-150 |
| Visura + planimetria catastale | must match reality; buyers' banks check | €1-20 per extract |
| Cadastral conformity | mismatched plans void financing and stall deeds | geometra update €300-1,500, 2-6 weeks |
| APE energy certificate | mandatory in ads and the deed | €150-300, 1 week |
| Building-permit history | proves works were authorised | comune access request, 2-8 weeks |
| Agibilità (where issued) | buyers discount its absence | duplicate request at the comune |
| Condominium rules + minutes (2 years) | reveals voted works and arrears | administrator, free-€50 |
| Mortgage payoff figure | cancellation choreography at closing | bank letter, 1-2 weeks |
| Utility and IMU positions | arrears surface in due diligence | statements |
| POA, if selling remotely | signature capacity | notarised + apostilled, 2-3 weeks |

The two chronic killers deserve emphasis: cadastral plans that do not match the actual layout, a moved kitchen, an enclosed balcony, and works without traceable permits. Both are fixable, neither is fixable quickly under an impatient offer, and both are exactly what a buyer's [due diligence](/guides/due-diligence-italy-property/) is designed to find.

**Insider tip:** commission your own pre-sale check from a geometra for €300-500 before photographs are even taken. Sellers who discover their own conformity defects choose the cheap fix on their own calendar; sellers who let the buyer discover them choose between a price cut and a dead deal on someone else's.

## How do Italian agency mandates and fees work for sellers?

Italian agencies work as dual intermediaries earning from both sides, and the seller's commission runs 2-4% plus 22% VAT, owed under the civil code the moment a concluded contract results from the agent's introduction, which in practice means at the accepted proposal, not at the rogito. Mandate architecture, more than fee haggling, determines your outcome.

The mandate decision in three moves:

1. **Esclusiva vs open.** A sole mandate of 4-6 months concentrates marketing spend and accountability; open mandates multiply listings with inconsistent prices and photos, a pattern buyers read as distress.
2. **Scope in writing.** Portal placement, professional photography, floorplans, viewing handling, and reporting cadence belong in the mandate text, not in conversation.
3. **Exit terms.** Tacit-renewal clauses and post-mandate commission tails (introduced buyers who return later) are the two lines to negotiate hardest.

| Mandate term | Market standard | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | 2-4% + VAT | dual intermediation is legal and normal |
| Duration | 4-6 months exclusive | auto-renewal beyond one cycle |
| Commission trigger | concluded deal (accepted proposal) | wording that survives buyer default |
| Tail period | 6-12 months | introduced-buyer lists, get them in writing |

Commission timing is the clause sellers most often misread, and the case law is settled against wishful readings: under article 1755 of the civil code the agent's right matures when a deal is concluded through their work, and Italian courts treat an accepted proposta d'acquisto as exactly that conclusion, so the 3% falls due months before the rogito and survives most later collapses of the deal. A seller who accepts an offer at €480,000 owes roughly €17,500 with VAT at that moment, even if the buyer's mortgage later fails, unless the mandate says otherwise. Two protective rewrites are market-acceptable: commission payable at compromesso rather than acceptance, and a clause voiding it where the deal fails on the buyer's documented financing condition. Agencies accept both more readily at mandate signature than ever again afterwards.

Pricing sits with you, not the agent: anchor to closed prices per square metre from the OMI zone data and recent comparables rather than to asking prices, because the asking-to-closed discount nationally has run high-single-digit for years. The buyer-side tactics in the [offer and negotiation guide](/guides/italy-property-offer-negotiation/) are exactly what will be used against your price, and reading them from the other chair is the cheapest pricing seminar available.

## What is the deal sequence from offer to rogito for a seller?

The sequence mirrors the buyer's but the risk profile inverts: accepting a proposta d'acquisto binds you first, the caparra confirmatoria binds you double, and your main exposure from compromesso onward is breach, where the buyer can demand twice the deposit back if you fail to convey. A seller's calendar runs 60-120 days from accepted offer to deed.

| Stage | Seller's obligations | Seller's risk if it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Proposta accepted | take property off market per terms | agent's commission is usually already earned |
| Compromesso + caparra 10-20% | deliver documents, maintain state | double-caparra refund on your breach |
| Between contracts | cooperate with surveys, clear arrears | price renegotiation on discovered defects |
| Rogito | deliver possession, keys, warranties | civil-code guarantees survive closing |

Mechanics worth pre-deciding as a seller rather than improvising: whether the caparra is confirmatoria or penitenziale (the difference decides whether either side can buy their way out, the [compromesso guide](/guides/compromesso-italy-property-contract/) maps it), whether you will accept a mortgage-conditional offer and on what deadline, and whether the buyer's deposito prezzo request is acceptable, which it should be, since it delays your funds only to registration, days not months, per the [notaio guide](/guides/notaio-italy-property-role/)'s timeline.

Warranty exposure survives the deed by law: hidden defects (vizi occulti) carry buyer action windows of 8 days from discovery within 1 year of delivery, and evicted-title guarantees run longer, which is one more argument for disclosure over concealment while negotiating.

## How is a foreign seller taxed on the sale?

Plusvalenza is binary on the calendar for most sellers: sell within 5 years of purchase and the gain, price minus documented purchase price and costs, is taxed at 26% by notarial election at the deed; sell after 5 years and the gain is simply not taxable income for a private individual, whatever its size. Two exceptions rewrite the clock.

The full decision table:

| Scenario | Tax outcome |
|---|---|
| Sale after 5 years, ordinary history | 0% on the gain |
| Sale within 5 years | 26% on net gain (or IRPEF marginal, rarely chosen) |
| Main residence for most of the holding | exempt even within 5 years |
| Inherited property | exempt regardless of timing |
| Superbonus works completed, sale within 10 years | 26% on gain, cost-base limits below |
| Non-resident seller | same rules; treaty credit questions belong to your home filing |

The 2024 superbonus rule needs its own arithmetic because it surprises otherwise-exempt sellers: sales within 10 years of completing 110%-style works are taxable at 26%, and where the works were financed by credit transfer or invoice discount, their cost is excluded from your cost base entirely for sales within 5 years of the works and counted at 50% between years 5 and 10. Inherited property and main homes for most of the period stay exempt. A Florence apartment bought at €300,000, superbonus-renovated in 2023 via sconto in fattura, and sold at €420,000 in 2027 is taxed on close to the full €120,000 spread, roughly €31,000 of substitute tax that a 2033 sale would avoid at 50% cost recognition and a 2034 sale entirely.

## Can you sell an Italian property without coming to Italy?

Remote sale is standard practice, not an exception: a procura speciale executed abroad, notarised, apostilled and paired with a sworn Italian translation costing €150-400, empowers a representative, a lawyer, a relative, even the notary's trusted collaborator, to sign both the compromesso and the rogito in your name, with your involvement reduced to video calls and couriered originals over roughly 3 weeks.

The remote seller's critical path adds 3 practical weeks:

1. **Draft the POA with the Italian notary first**: the text must name the property by cadastral identifiers and enumerate powers (sell, set price floor, receive or direct payment, settle taxes).
2. **Execute locally**: notarisation, apostille under the 1961 Hague Convention (consular legalisation where no apostille applies).
3. **Translate**: sworn translation in Italy; budget €150-400.
4. **Courier originals**: the Italian notary needs wet-ink or digitally authenticated originals on file.

Payment routing deserves one explicit decision: proceeds by assegno circolare require someone to bank the draft in Italy, so remote sellers usually prefer transfer to their Italian account with onward SEPA/SWIFT, or direct transfer with pre-cleared compliance at the receiving bank. The full mechanics, including the deposito prezzo's interaction with remote closings, are in the [power of attorney guide](/guides/power-of-attorney-property-italy/).

**Insider tip:** grant the POA a price floor, not a fixed price («no less than €X net of commissions»). Fixed-price POAs die when negotiation moves €5,000, and each redraft costs another apostille-translation-courier cycle measured in weeks.

Seller scenarios split the playbook three ways. The post-5-year holiday-home owner has the simplest file: no plusvalenza, a clean tax position, and preparation is purely documentary. The 3-year owner selling early should model the 26% substitute tax against waiting out the clock, on a €60,000 gain, patience is worth €15,600, which reframes any urgency the agent projects. And the superbonus-renovated owner faces the 10-year rule's arithmetic explicitly: rushing a 2027 sale versus structuring a 2033 one can swing the net by tens of thousands, making the sale date itself the single most valuable clause in their plan.

## What does the sell-side timeline and budget actually total?

A prepared foreign seller should plan 6-9 months end-to-end and 3-5% of price in costs: 1-2 months of file preparation running in parallel with marketing launch, the national-average 5-7 months to an acceptable offer, then 2-4 months of contract sequence, with the cost stack dominated by the agency line and everything else in the hundreds.

| Cost line | Typical amount |
|---|---|
| Agency commission | 2-4% + 22% VAT |
| APE certificate | €150-300 |
| Cadastral/permit fixes (if needed) | €300-1,500 |
| Mortgage cancellation | €500-1,000 |
| POA + apostille + translation (remote) | €400-900 |
| Plusvalenza (only if inside the clocks) | 26% of net gain |
| **Typical all-in, clean file, post-5-year sale** | **≈3-5% of price** |

Sequenced as a checklist, the whole guide compresses to seven moves: assemble the file and fix conformity first; commission the APE; choose one agency on a 4-6 month esclusiva with written scope; price from closed comparables; pre-decide caparra type, financing conditions and deposito prezzo stance; prepare the POA chain if selling remotely; and elect the 26% substitute at the deed only after checking both the 5-year and superbonus 10-year clocks against your dates. Sellers who run the list in that order routinely beat the average timeline by months, and the ones who do not usually meet this guide's contents one discovered defect at a time.
