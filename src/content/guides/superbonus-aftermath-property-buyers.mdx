Quick answer: the superbonus renovated a visible slice of Italy's housing at 110% of cost and left the market split in two: completed, documented projects that are the best value stock of the decade, and buildings carrying open credits, contractor claims or unfinished sites that transfer their problems to buyers through the condominio. The sorting instrument is a 4-document check through the amministratore costing €0-100, plus the 10-year resale tax rule that shapes every seller's calendar. This guide covers what the scheme did to the stock, the credit mechanics that can bite, the building-level due diligence, and pricing both halves, with the energy-class economics in the [APE guide](/guides/energy-class-ape-italy-property/).

## What did the superbonus actually do to the housing stock?

The superbonus was the largest renovation subsidy in Italian history: from 2020 it funded qualifying energy and seismic works at 110% of cost, the credits became sellable paper through cessione and sconto in fattura, and by the scheme's wind-down the state had accrued well over €120 billion of credits across hundreds of thousands of buildings, condominio projects above all.

What that means physically in today's listings:

| Legacy | Where you see it | Buyer relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Deep-retrofitted condominios | new façades, cappotto insulation, 2019-era G stock now B-C | premium stock, check the file |
| Seismic-improved buildings | zones 1-3, structural works with asseverazioni | value where documented |
| Mid-scheme orphans | scaffolding still up, works halted 2023-2024 | deep discounts, deep risk |
| Credit-burdened buildings | litigation lines in minutes, unpaid contractor claims | priced as liabilities |

The scheme's name still moves listings: «ristrutturato con superbonus» appears in thousands of adverts as a pure quality claim, and it genuinely correlates with new systems and warm walls. What it does not certify is the paperwork's state, which is the entire difference between the two halves of this guide.

Two numbers frame every file you will meet:

- **110% meant 0% owner cost** on compliant projects, so sellers of completed stock genuinely received their retrofit free, and their pricing psychology reflects it.
- **The 2-class jump requirement** baked energy improvement into the subsidy's design, which is why completed projects moved stock straight into the [APE](/guides/energy-class-ape-italy-property/) bands the market now pays premiums for.

Fiscal scale is what makes the aftermath a durable market feature rather than a passing story: the accrued credits exceeded €120 billion against an original estimate near €35 billion, parliamentary reviews put the cost overrun at more than 3 times the plan, and the wind-down laws of 2023-2024 rewrote transfer rules 20+ times by practitioners' counts. Each rewrite stranded someone mid-chain: an owner between SALs, a contractor holding unsellable paper, a bank pausing acquisitions to reprice. The stock consequences will outlive the litigation by decades, because the physical works, roughly 500,000 building interventions by programme counts, are permanent, while the paper positions resolve one building at a time. A buyer's job is never to judge the programme: it is to establish which side of its ledger one specific building landed on.

## How did the credit mechanics work, and where did they break?

The scheme's engine was transferable paper: an owner's 110% tax credit could be sold to banks (cessione del credito) or traded to the contractor as a discount (sconto in fattura), and the system ran smoothly until credit demand saturated in 2022-2023, transfer rules changed repeatedly, and billions in credits became incagliati: earned, asseverated, and unsellable.

The failure map, from the buyer's chair:

1. **Blocked credits (crediti incagliati)**: contractors holding paper they could not monetise stopped paying subcontractors or halted sites; the building's project stalled with them.
2. **Clawback exposure**: credits found non-compliant (inflated invoices, missing asseverazioni) are recovered from the owners who benefited, with the building's then-owners in the chain.
3. **Contractor insolvency**: firms that financed works against future credit sales failed when the market froze, leaving claims and liens around otherwise finished buildings.
4. **General-contractor disputes**: fixed-price «free renovation» contracts turned litigious when rule changes moved the economics mid-project.

Geography and building size shaped where the breakage concentrated: condominio projects, the scheme's biggest spenders, needed general contractors able to finance €1-3 million of works against future credit sales, so when the transfer market froze in 2022-2023 the mid-sized firms that had grown 10x in 2 years were the first to stall, and the buildings they were wrapping held the scaffolding. Single-villa projects, smaller and often bank-financed conventionally, closed at far higher rates. The practical screening consequence: a renovated villa's file is usually a formality check, while a renovated 20-unit condominio's file is the due diligence, and the bigger the project the more the final SAL's date matters, because sites that reached 2024 unfinished did so into the hardest refinancing market of the cycle.

The sorting question for any specific building is binary and documentary: is the file closed. A project whose final SAL is certified, whose asseverazioni sit in the ENEA registry and whose credits were accepted by a named bank is history you benefit from; every open item on that list is a live position you would co-own through the [condominio](/guides/condominio-rules-foreign-owners/), and it prices like one.

**Insider tip:** ask the amministratore one precise question in writing: «esiste un contenzioso o un credito non ceduto relativo ai lavori 110%?». The written answer is either a clean bill you can file with your deed papers, or the first document of a negotiation the listing never mentioned.

## What is the building-level due diligence for superbonus stock?

The check runs through the amministratore, costs €0-100 in copy fees, and takes a week: four document sets establish whether the project is a closed benefit or an open liability, and 24 months of assembly minutes then catch whatever the formal file omits, from simmering claims to the vote scheduling the works' warranty enforcement.

| Document | What closed looks like | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Works resolution + contract | costs borne via sconto/cessione, scope defined | owner top-ups unresolved, scope disputes |
| SAL chain (states of advancement) | final SAL certified, site closed | last SAL partial, works «suspended» |
| Asseverazioni + ENEA filings | filed, numbers verifiable | missing, «being corrected» |
| Credit position | accepted by named transferee, no residue | incagliato residue, contractor claims |

The minutes then answer the human questions: whether the assembly is fighting the contractor, whether owners face top-up calls after rule changes, whether the two-year defect window from the [warranty regime](/guides/italy-new-build-warranty-defects/) is being enforced or slept through. On completed projects the same minutes usually record the pleasant version: acceptance, warranty letters, and a building whose next facade cycle just moved 25 years away.

Asseverazione checks deserve their own line because they anchor the clawback risk: every superbonus project required sworn technical attestations (congruity of costs, energy jump, seismic classes where relevant) filed with ENEA or the professional registries, the attestations carried €50,000-scale professional insurance per signatory, and the revenue agency's audit campaigns target exactly the projects whose filings are missing, late or internally inconsistent. For a buyer the practical test is retrievability: an amministratore who produces the asseverazioni and ENEA receipts within 5 working days evidences a project run by professionals, while «the contractor has them» repeated after 3 separate written requests describes a file that may well not survive an audit, and the audit's clawback lands on the owners of record, split by millesimi, whoever they are by then.

Unit-level checks stay ordinary but gain one addition: the works should appear in the unit's paper trail coherently (CILAS filings, updated APE, any variation in cadastral data), and the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-italy-property/)'s conformity pass will surface mismatches between what the subsidy paperwork claims and what the walls show.

## How should buyers price the two halves of the legacy?

Pricing splits on the file's state, and the market already grades it: completed-and-closed superbonus stock trades at 5-15% premiums to unrenovated twins (still below replacement economics, since the works cost €50,000-100,000 per unit at 2021-2023 prices), while open-position buildings discount 10-30% and orphaned sites deeper still, discounts that are compensation, not bargains, until priced against the exposure.

A Bologna worked comparison on 85 sqm in Bolognina:

| Asset | Price | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Unrenovated 1965 flat, class F | €210,000 | retrofit ahead at your cost |
| Same building type, closed 110% file, class B | €235,000 | works done, warranties running, file clean |
| Similar stock, credits in dispute | €185,000 | €25,000 «saving» against an unquantified claim |

Reading the table with discipline:

1. **The €25,000 premium for the closed file** buys roughly €70,000 of executed works plus a decade of façade peace: the cheapest renovation you will ever not do.
2. **The €25,000 discount on the disputed building** is only a price after a lawyer reads the claim: condominio liabilities split by millesimi, and your share of an unpaid €400,000 contractor claim can eat the discount twice.
3. **The unrenovated flat** is the honest benchmark: its economics are the [renovation guide](/guides/italy-property-renovation-costs-guide/)'s territory, now at 36-50% detrazioni rather than 110%.

Discount arithmetic on disputed buildings needs one worked example to stop being abstract: a 20-unit Bologna condominio carries a €400,000 unpaid contractor claim from a halted 110% site, litigation running 3 years with a plausible settlement at 60%, so the building's realistic exposure is €240,000 plus €40,000-60,000 of legal costs, call it €15,000 per average unit by millesimi. A flat inside offered €25,000 under clean comparables is therefore priced €10,000 ahead of the exposure midpoint, before valuing 3 years of assemblies about lawyers and a facade that stays wrapped meanwhile. The same flat at €45,000 under comparables is a rational value trade for a buyer whose counsel confirms the numbers. The market's discounts are information, and the only mistake is accepting them unquantified.

Seller psychology completes the pricing picture: owners of closed-file stock received their retrofit free and often anchor to fantasy premiums, while the 10-year resale tax rule (26% on gains, with works costs excluded from basis within 5 years of completion and half-counted to year 10) gives many of them a real calendar incentive to wait, or to accept price for speed, mechanics the [capital gains guide](/guides/italy-capital-gains-tax-property/) details and a negotiator can use from the [selling guide](/guides/selling-property-italy-foreigner/)'s other chair.

## What are the scenarios where superbonus stock fits, and where it does not?

Buyer scenarios sort the legacy cleanly: the yield investor targeting corporate lets wants the closed-file B-class stock and pays its premium, the value buyer with legal appetite can underwrite disputed buildings at 20%+ discounts after counsel prices the claims, and the risk-averse holiday buyer should simply require a clean file as a condition of offer and walk where it is refused.

The decision grid by profile:

| Profile | Right half of the legacy | Deal condition to insist on |
|---|---|---|
| Rental investor | closed-file retrofits | written credit-position confirmation |
| Renovator | unrenovated stock (skip the legacy) | ordinary conformity checks |
| Value hunter with counsel | disputed buildings at 20-30% off | lawyer's quantification of exposure |
| Lifestyle buyer | closed-file only | clean-file clause in the proposta |

Financing interacts with the file's state one more time at the bank: lenders' appraisers discount or decline collateral inside disputed or unfinished buildings, several banks keep internal blacklists of stalled 110% sites, and a mortgage-dependent buyer should treat the clean file as a financing condition, not only a price input. Cash buyers hold the structural advantage in the disputed half of this market for exactly that reason, and they should price it into their offers rather than give it away.

Timing adds a fourth lever for every profile: buildings resolve their positions continuously (credits accepted, settlements signed, sites completed by replacement contractors), and a disputed building revisited 12 months later is often a clean building whose discount has halved. Buyers not in a hurry can shortlist disputed stock, diarise the amministratore's updates, and buy the resolution rather than the risk, a patience trade that consistently beats bidding against the clean-file premium in the same street.

The one scenario with no good version is buying into an orphaned site: scaffolding rent accruing, contractor insolvent, credits clawed back and works 70% complete is a corporate restructuring wearing a residential address, and the sub-market prices that truth with discounts that still undercompensate the median buyer's capacity to manage it. Between the extremes, the instrument that moves value is always the same: the written state of the file, obtained before the offer, priced without sentiment, and attached to the [conditions](/guides/italy-property-offer-negotiation/) that make it enforceable. The wider machinery of eligibility, contracts and closing stays in the [foreign buyer hub](/guides/buy-property-italy-foreigner/).
