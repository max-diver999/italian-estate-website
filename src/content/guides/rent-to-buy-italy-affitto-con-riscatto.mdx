Quick answer: affitto con riscatto lets you move into an Italian home now, pay a monthly canone of which a contract-defined share, commonly 40-60%, builds toward a locked purchase price, and complete the purchase within a term of usually 3-5 years. A 2014 statute gives the scheme teeth: the notarised contract is registered against the property, shielding you from the seller's creditors and any resale for up to 10 years. This guide covers the mechanics, the walk-away economics on both sides, taxes during the rental phase, and where the scheme beats a [non-resident mortgage](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-italy/) or an [off-plan stage plan](/guides/italy-off-plan-property-guide/).

## What is rent-to-buy under Italian law?

Affitto con riscatto is a typed contract from article 23 of the 2014 Sblocca Italia decree: immediate possession, a canone split between use and price account, and a right, or an obligation if the parties prefer, to complete the purchase at a price fixed on day one, within a term the transcription protects for up to 10 years.

The structure in sequence:

1. **Notarised contract**: states the price, the term, the canone split, and whether purchase is an option or an obligation.
2. **Transcription**: the notary registers the contract like a deed; your priority runs from this date.
3. **Occupancy phase**: you live in (or let, if agreed) the home; credits accumulate monthly.
4. **Rogito**: the final deed transfers title; accumulated credits reduce the balance due.

| Feature | Value in the standard scheme |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | art. 23 DL 133/2014, conv. L. 164/2014 |
| Maximum protected term | 10 years (transcription) |
| Price | fixed at signature |
| Purchase | option (default) or obligation (by clause) |
| Registers effect | opposable to later buyers, mortgages, creditors |

## How is the canone split between rent and price?

The split is free contract territory with hard consequences: the godimento share is economically rent and stays with the owner in every scenario, while the acconto prezzo share is a purchase credit whose fate on walk-away the contract itself must fix. Italian practice clusters the price share at 40-60% of the monthly payment.

A worked Bari structure on a €250,000 apartment over 4 years:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly canone | €1,250 |
| Use component (godimento) | €600 |
| Price-account component | €650 |
| Credits after 48 months | €31,200 |
| Fixed purchase price | €250,000 |
| Balance at rogito | €218,800 |

Two structural notes keep the arithmetic honest:

- The use component should track market rent for comparable stock; a use share far below market invites requalification arguments and prices the seller's patience unrealistically.
- The price component is not interest-bearing: €31,200 of credits is worth exactly €31,200 at rogito, so long terms trade convenience against inflation on your own money.

**Insider tip:** benchmark the fixed price against today's value plus a stated growth assumption, and write the comparison into your file. A seller pricing 4 years of expected growth into the fixed price while also collecting market-level godimento is charging twice for the same time.

## What protection does transcription actually give?

Transcription is the scheme's spine: the notarised contract enters the property registers with the same publicity machinery as a sale, making the future buyer's right opposable to whoever comes later, a second buyer, a new mortgage, or the seller's creditors, with priority from the transcription date and a shield lasting up to 10 years.

What this defeats, concretely:

1. **Resale to a third party**: a later buyer takes subject to your registered right; your completion beats their deed.
2. **New mortgages**: a bank lending against the property after your transcription ranks behind you.
3. **Creditor attachment**: a pignoramento transcribed after your contract does not sweep away your position.
4. **Seller bankruptcy**: the standard residential contract survives; the trustee cannot simply dissolve it against a transcribed occupier-buyer.

Transcription mechanics reward precision about dates: priority runs from the day the notary files the contract in the conservatoria, not from signature, and every right transcribed afterwards, a buyer's deed, a bank's ipoteca, a creditor's pignoramento, ranks behind it for up to 10 years. The occupier-buyer therefore holds what amounts to a security interest without ever being a lender: on a €250,000 Bari contract with €31,200 of scheduled credits, the registers protect both the future title and, through the contract's own terms, the credited money, at a filing cost of a few hundred euros folded into the notary's overall bill for the deed. Ordinary tenants enjoy nothing comparable, and even holders of an unregistered compromesso stand exposed for 100% of their caparra against exactly the events this filing defeats.

The comparison that matters for buyers choosing instruments: an unregistered private compromesso gives none of this, and even a registered preliminare's shield runs shorter, the mechanics covered in the [compromesso guide](/guides/compromesso-italy-property-contract/). Rent-to-buy pairs the longest registered protection available to a future buyer with actual possession, which is why credit-building purchasers use it as a bridge toward the mortgage file a [non-resident application](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-italy/) will demand.

## What are the walk-away economics on each side?

Walking away costs the occupier the use component in full and, typically, a contract-set share of the price credits: the statute expressly allows the seller to retain part as indemnity, and Italian deeds commonly fix retention at 50% or more. The seller's walk-away, refusing to convey, triggers ordinary breach remedies including specific performance.

The Bari example's exit table after 30 months:

| Scenario | Occupier's position | Owner's position |
|---|---|---|
| Completes purchase | €19,500 credits offset the price | receives balance, deal done |
| Walks away (50% retention) | recovers €9,750 of €19,500 | keeps €9,750 + all godimento |
| Owner refuses to sell | sues for specific performance (art. 2932) | faces forced transfer + damages |
| Owner's creditor attacks | transcription priority holds | irrelevant to occupier |

Reading the table as a negotiation map: retention percentage is the single most negotiable number in the deed, and it prices the option you hold. A 100% retention clause converts your price credits into an option premium, rational only if the fixed price sits well below expected value; a 0% retention makes walking away nearly free and will be priced against you elsewhere. The [offer and negotiation guide](/guides/italy-property-offer-negotiation/)'s framework applies to this clause with more force than to the headline price.

## How is the rental phase taxed?

Taxation follows the split: the use component is taxed to the owner as rental income, eligible for cedolare secca at 21% where the owner qualifies, while the price-account component is not income at all but a debt movement, taxed only at the rogito inside the price. Registration duty during the phase runs on the lease logic for the use share.

| Item | During the phase | At rogito |
|---|---|---|
| Use component | owner's rental income (cedolare 21% possible) | n/a |
| Price credits | no income tax; 3% registration as price advances (private seller), credited later | absorbed into price |
| IMU | owner pays (owner's asset) | buyer's from transfer |
| Purchase taxes | n/a | ordinary: 9%/2% registration on cadastral value, or VAT if seller is a company |
| Occupier's costs | utilities, ordinary condominium fees | ordinary closing costs |

Fiscal treatment of the two canone components diverged cleanly once the revenue agency's 2015 circular settled the scheme's open questions: the godimento share follows lease taxation from day one, meaning 21% cedolare secca for qualifying private owners, while the acconto share is treated as a price advance, registering at 3% for private-seller deals with the paid duty credited against the final registration bill at rogito. Nothing about the phase disturbs the buyer's later entitlements: prezzo-valore still applies to an individual completing on residential stock, first-home rates still apply where residency conditions are met within 18 months of the transfer, and the 5-year capital-gains clock starts at the rogito, not at occupancy. The one figure worth diarising is the credit: 3% paid during the phase is money recovered at completion, but only if the deed's paper trail names it.

The rogito itself is an ordinary closing: prezzo-valore applies for individual buyers of residential stock, the balance moves under the traceability rules described in the [money transfer guide](/guides/transferring-money-to-italy-property/), and the closing-cost stack mirrors the [cost of buying guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-italy/) minus the credits already parked. Sellers should note the 3% registration on price-account advances from private deals is creditable against the final registration bill, a cash-flow wrinkle, not a double tax.

## When does rent-to-buy beat a mortgage or off-plan plan?

Rent-to-buy earns its complexity in three buyer situations: a deposit 2-3 years short of mortgage thresholds, a non-resident credit file Italian banks cannot yet read, and a seller-financed price in markets where banks lend reluctantly on the stock itself, historic centres and rural builds alike. Against each alternative the trade is specific.

| Alternative | Where it wins | Where rent-to-buy wins |
|---|---|---|
| Non-resident mortgage now | rates locked, immediate title, leverage | no bank test today; 3-5 years to build the file |
| Saving while renting | flexibility, no walk-away cost | price locked against growth; credits build automatically |
| Off-plan stage payments | new stock, bank-guaranteed advances | existing homes; occupy from day one |
| Vendor loan (rare) | simplicity | statutory protection + registration machinery |

Market sizing explains why sellers accept the structure at all: Italian banks approve roughly half of non-resident mortgage applications, stock outside prime centres can sit unsold for 8-12 months, and a seller facing a €250,000 asking price with thin demand often nets more from 4 years of godimento plus a full-price completion than from a −10% immediate sale. Rent-to-buy converts a stale listing into paid optionality on both sides: the seller collects €600 a month of use income while keeping the sale alive, the buyer locks price and possession while building the file a bank will eventually read. The structure's revival after the 2014 statute tracked exactly those frictions, and it remains most common where they bite hardest, southern cities, historic centres, and the non-resident segment this site serves.

The honest disqualifiers, stated plainly:

1. Buyers certain of completing and bankable today: a mortgage is cheaper; credits pay no interest while a repayment builds equity.
2. Markets falling or flat: a fixed price transfers downside to you; renegotiate or prefer renting.
3. Sellers needing sale proceeds now: the structure fails their side and dies in negotiation anyway.

Fit confirmed, the sequence runs: agree the split and retention, notarise, transcribe, diarise the option deadline, and treat the rogito preparation with the same rigour as the [step-by-step guide](/guides/how-to-buy-italy-property-step-by-step/) prescribes for a direct purchase, because that is exactly what the final act is.

Occupier scenarios sort the scheme's value cleanly. The deposit-builder, 2-3 years from a 20% down payment, extracts the most: locked price, forced saving through the acconto share, and a credit file growing with every documented canone. The non-resident credit-builder gains the Italian banking history a future mortgage application needs, provided every payment runs by transfer with a clean causale. The market-timer hoping to lock today's price against a rising city pays for that view twice, in godimento and in interest-free credits, and should verify the view against actual OMI zone trends rather than agency optimism before signing a 4-year commitment.

## What clauses decide whether the contract is safe?

Contract quality decides 100% of the hard cases in this scheme, because the statute fixes the skeleton and leaves the economic joints to the parties: retention percentage, split ratio, term, option deadline mechanics, cost allocation, letting rights, indexation, and default cure periods. Each of the eight has a market-standard answer worth demanding before signature.

The clause checklist, with the positions that protect the buyer:

1. **Retention on walk-away**: cap at 50% of price credits; resist 100% unless the fixed price is visibly below market.
2. **Option deadline**: a date plus a notice mechanism (PEC or notarised letter), never a bare date.
3. **Cost allocation**: tenant-pattern costs to you, IMU and extraordinary repairs to the owner, in writing.
4. **Letting**: permission if your plans may change; silence defaults against you.
5. **Default cure**: the statute tolerates missed canoni up to a twentieth of their number; tighten or confirm, never worsen.
6. **Price traceability**: every credit paid by transfer with causale naming the contract; cash credits are unprovable credits.
7. **Condition surveys**: entry and pre-rogito states documented, since you will have lived in your own future purchase for years.

Legal review before signature costs €1,500-3,000 with a notary involved from the draft stage, roughly 1% of the Bari example's price, and it is the only line in this guide that buys protection rather than convenience: the scheme's decade of registered priority is only as strong as the deed that earns it.
