## Which Bologna districts offer the best university rental returns?

University returns typically concentrate in San Donato, Irnerio, and Mazzini corridors at €3,100 to €3,900/m² with gross yields near 3.8% to 5.0% on renovated bilocale stock serving 90,000 enrolments in 2026. Model voids under four weeks when fiber and quiet workspace meet researcher standards.

Student demand in Bologna is dense enough to distort a district's economics, and the returns follow proximity to faculties rather than to the Piazza Maggiore. The Zamboni quarter and the streets around Porta San Vitale sit inside the historic core with the highest per sqm cost and the shortest letting cycles; San Donato and Savena offer the value entry with the same academic-year demand at a longer walk or a bus ride; Navile combines regeneration stock with campus access. Against a citywide average near €3,700 per sqm, the value corridors return 3.5-4.5% gross where the centro trophy stock returns less. The variable that decides an individual building is the lease structure: an academic-year contract leaves a summer gap, while a twelve-month furnished lease to a postgraduate or a researcher removes it and is worth accepting a lower headline rent to secure.

## How strong is the hospital corridor rental market in Bologna?

The Policlinico Sant'Orsola corridor produces a tenant Bologna landlords value above almost any other: consultants and fellows signing furnished leases of three to four years. Stock in Murri and Costa Saragozza trades at €3,800-4,500 per sqm and returns 3.2-4.0% gross — modest on paper, but with near-zero vacancy.

Screen the lift, the garage and the broadband before marketing to that tenant, because all three are treated as requirements rather than extras.

## What rental yields can Bologna property investors expect in 2026?

Bologna rental yields typically range from 2.5% to 3.5% in centro trophies up to 4.8% in Navile regeneration on furnished twelve-month leases after IMU near 0.76% in 2026 models. Nets cedolare secca at 21% before foreign-buyer shortlists leave the desk.

| Navile furnished LTR | 2,865-4,000 | 3.5-4.8% | 2.5-3.5% |

Net yield calculation must include IMU on second homes at 0.86-1.06% of cadastral value depending on luxury thresholds, condominium ordinary and extraordinary spese, maintenance reserve at 5-8% of gross rent, and cedolare secca at 21% on first-property long leases or 26% on additional properties. Non-residents electing ordinary IRPEF face different mechanics; model with commercialista before compromesso.

A worked example: €300,000 Navile bilocale, €1,100 monthly furnished rent, €13,200 annual gross equals 4.4% gross. After €2,400 IMU and condominium, €1,500 maintenance, and €2,772 cedolare secca at 21%, net cash approximates €6,528 or 2.18% net yield before mortgage interest. That math beats centro trophy net on similar capex but trails Puglia yield plays; Bologna investors trade spread for employment stability and AV rail optionality.

Bologna yields are unspectacular and unusually reliable, which is the whole case for the city. Furnished long leases across the university and hospital corridors return 3.5-4.5% gross, with Navile regeneration stock at the upper end and centro portico trophy property below it. What distinguishes those numbers from a coastal market quoting 6-8% is what sits behind them: a permanent student population near 90,000, the Sant'Orsola hospital complex, and high-speed rail placing Milan and Florence inside 40 minutes, none of which pauses in February. A €312,000 Navile bilocale therefore models with vacancy under a month rather than with a five-month void. Against that, short-let upside is limited by tightening rules in new towers, so a buyer choosing Bologna is explicitly trading peak-season income for occupancy certainty across the year.

## What share of Bologna buyers are foreign and how do they participate?

Foreign buyers take roughly 15-22% of prime Bologna rogiti, with British, American and German investors typically borrowing at around 60% loan-to-value from Italian banks and electing cedolare secca at 21% on qualifying long leases. On a non-EU file, reciprocity clearance and the codice fiscale both need to be in place before the caparra moves, not after — a delay there is the most common cause of a missed completion date.

Italian domestic buyers still dominate centro portico rogiti, which supports resale liquidity when foreign owners price realistically for local comparables rather than Milan or London mental anchors.

EU citizens purchase freely with codice fiscale, Italian bank account, and notary-led rogito. Banks use lower of purchase price or perizia appraisal, which matters less in Bologna than Milan bidding wars but still affects Navile off-plan milestone financing. Budget equity for Bloom Living-style schedules with notary escrow on caparra confirmatoria.

## Underwriting snapshot

Bologna underwriting rests on tenant depth rather than tourism, which is what separates it from most Italian city cases. Buyers from abroad should model a 12-month furnished lease to university, hospital or corporate tenants, then subtract an IMU charge between 0.86% and 1.06% of cadastral value and 21% cedolare secca.

**Insider tip:** Navile sellers often bundle parking deeds and Class A energy certificates when listings sit 90+ days through winter; verify AV noise buffers on track-facing units before marketing walking minutes to Bologna Centrale.

Budget between 10% and 15% all-in on a non-resident second-home rogito before underwriting net yield.

Underwriting snapshot: Bologna is a long-lease market, so 21% cedolare secca rather than the 26% short-let rate is the working assumption. Add 9% registration tax on a second home and the comune IMU band, and the fixed side of the model is complete.
