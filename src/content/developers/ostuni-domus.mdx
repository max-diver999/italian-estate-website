## What is the rental market outlook for this format?

Ostuni pool villas in the €400k-500k band often achieve €80-120 nightly in peak season with 60-70% summer occupancy under professional management. The market for this size is families rather than groups: a 150 sqm three-bedroom villa competes on photography and pool quality, not on headcount, which caps weekly rates well below the large-villa segment.

Weekly family bookings can exceed €700-900 in July-August when properties photograph well and include pools with sunset terraces.

Gross yields of 4-6% annually appear achievable on €470,000 tickets blending STR summer and occasional long-term winter leases. Net yields fall after management (often 20-25%), cleaning, platform fees, and cedolare secca.

Long-term furnished leases to remote workers or expat residents run €800-1,200 monthly in winter, lower than Milan but with lower void risk if STR is not desired.

## What are the running costs on a €470,000 pool villa?

Holding costs on this format run to roughly €3,300-€4,800 a year before management fees, and the pool is the line that separates it from an apartment. IMU on a countryside second home, utilities on 150 sqm, pool servicing and insurance are the fixed four; landscaping is optional only until the garden is part of the rental listing.

| Cost line | Indicative annual |
|-----------|-----------------|
| IMU | €1,200-1,500 |
| Utilities | €800-1,200 |
| Pool maintenance | €600-800 |
| Insurance | €300-500 |
| Garden / landscaping | €400-800 optional |

Running costs on a rural pool villa are structurally different from an apartment's, because almost nothing is shared. IMU on a second home falls in the 0.86-1.06% band on cadastral value, and there are no condominium spese to spread the load. Against that sit costs an apartment owner never meets: pool maintenance and chemicals across the season, grounds and olive management, an independent water supply or borehole to maintain, and insurance on a property standing empty for much of the winter. Where the villa is let, management runs 20-25% of gross and cleaning is charged per changeover. A realistic all-in annual figure on a €470,000 villa lands well above what a comparable Italian apartment costs to hold, which is why the yield case should be modelled net rather than gross from the outset.

## What should be verified before closing?

Before compromesso on Ostuni Domus turnkey stock, verify inclusion list, handover timeline, and warranty transfer in writing.

**€470,000 inclusion audit.** Confirm pool, landscaping, parking, kitchen appliances, and air conditioning in the sales bundle. Turnkey promoters sometimes exclude gates, outdoor lighting, or pool equipment from base quotes.

**Handover timing.** Q3 2026 targets require habitability certificate readiness and snagging resolution before rogito balance. Near-completion buyers should inspect finish quality versus showroom renders physically.

**Warranty and defects.** Request developer warranty terms on structural envelope, MEP, and pool installation. Document snagging items with photos before final payment tranche.

**Condominium and STR.** Obtain draft regolamento if any shared road or gate costs apply. Verify CIN registration path for pool villa STR before underwriting summer revenue projections.

**Tax setup.** Model registration tax at 2% prima casa or 9% second home and annual IMU near €1,200-1,500 on €470,000 countryside villas. Budget 8-10% total buyer costs including notary.

**Pool compliance.** Verify pool registration with local health authorities where required and confirm filtration equipment specifications match Brindisi province standards for new private pools on countryside plots.

**Furnishing timeline.** If targeting summer 2026 STR launch, align rogito date with furnishing lead times. Turnkey has no statutory definition in Italy, so the word tells you nothing until you have read the capitolato. On the €470,000 Ostuni format the base contract typically covers the structure, roof, external render, glazing, internal partitions, bathroom and kitchen connections, and the mechanical and electrical first fix. What sits outside it, more often than buyers expect, is the pool and its SCIA, boundary walls and gates, the access track, landscaping, and the connection charges for water and power on a rural plot. Each of those is a real cost with its own filing, and together they can add a material share to the headline. The practical test is a line-by-line specification sheet before any reservation, with the pool treated as a separate project rather than an inclusion. A fixed price is only fixed against a defined scope.
