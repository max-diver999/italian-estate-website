## Overview

The Langhe is a UNESCO-inscribed vineyard landscape south of Alba where Barolo and Barbaresco carry global recognition and truffle season fills the autumn. What it does not have is Turin's year-round tenant depth: agriturismo and short-let calendars here go quiet from November to March. Restored cascine with documented conformità anchor resale far better than raw renovation shells marketed with drone photography before any SCIA has been filed, particularly above €650,000.

## Property Prices and Village Bands

Langhe village stock spans roughly €1,800-4,500 per sqm depending on vineyard views and the standard of restoration, with premium cascine in the Barolo and Barbaresco communes reaching €3,500-5,000 on hospitality-ready properties. Portal asking averages overshoot winter closings by 8-12%, and the comparable set in any single village is small enough that three closed sales in the same micro-market are essential before anchoring an offer.

| Village / zone | Indicative €/m² | Character | Inventory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barolo premium | €3,500-5,000+ | Trophy vineyard views | Very thin |
| Barbaresco | €3,200-4,800 | Hospitality-ready cascine | Thin |
| La Morra / Monforte | €2,500-3,800 | Mid-premium hill stock | Medium |
| Castiglione / Serralunga | €1,800-2,800 | Value renovation | Medium |
| Inland no-view comuni | €1,500-2,200 | Capex plays | Thin |

1. Anchor offers to three village closed sales before harvest season listing peaks.
2. Expect 10 to 15 percent asking premium over winter closes on Barolo premium micro-markets.
3. Compare inland sub-€2,200 per sqm tickets only with full renovation capex budgets attached.

## Rental Yields and Hospitality Profiles

Two income routes, and they differ in labour more than in return. A licensed agriturismo on a well-operated cascina reaches 4-6% gross seasonally before management labour is priced in; a long lease to wine-sector staff returns 3-4% with a fraction of the intensity, on tickets above €650,000.

Foreign buyers should model net cash flow after IMU, staffing, and platform fees because passive owners without Italian teams should underwrite long-lease or lifestyle hold rather than unmanaged STR on identical €720,000 cascina purchases.

1. Model net yield after staffing at 25 to 35 percent of gross on agriturismo operations.
2. Attach SCIA and CIN path confirmation before marketing hospitality-ready cascine to foreign enquiry.
3. Set yield math against [Langhe Barolo vineyard property](/projects/langhe-barolo-vineyard-property/) flagship ticket.

## How does the Langhe connect to Milan, Turin, and Alba?

Barolo village is 70-90 minutes from Milan by car and 50-70 from Turin, with Alba 15-40 minutes away for daily services and healthcare. There is no commuter rail from the hill villages, and the last stretch to many cascine runs on unsealed roads. On €650,000-plus tickets that is a genuine constraint for owners splitting weeks between a Milan office and a vineyard residence — the drive is the commute.

| Destination | Approximate time | Investor use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alba centro services | 15-40 min car | Groceries, healthcare |
| Turin | 50-70 min car | Weekend base link |
| Milan | 70-90 min car | Split-week commuter |
| Barolo to La Morra | 5-15 min car | Village hopping |

1. Car dependency is mandatory for hill cascine without urban tenant depth assumptions.
2. Alba adjacency balances remote hill privacy with urban services within 15 to 40 minutes.
3. Milan split-week owners model furnished lease fallback in Alba when agriturismo voids winter months.

## Foreign Buyer Practicalities

Foreign buyer practicalities in the Langhe hinge on land classification rather than nationality: residential structures transfer normally, but agricultural parcels attached to a cascina above €650,000 need commercialista and avvocato review before offer. Restoration of raw stone stock runs €150,000-€350,000 before SCIA, CIN and agriturismo licensing complete, so sequence the licence path before the capex.
