## Quick Answer

Portofino is a village of roughly 400 residents on the Tigullio Gulf whose harbour-facing apartments trade at €12,000-18,000 per sqm, with interior units at €8,000-11,000. Licensed short-let returns 2-3% gross on tickets above €1,200,000, so nobody buys here for cash flow — they buy a globally recognised address with almost no replacement supply. Santa Margherita Ligure, ten minutes away at €4,000-8,000 per sqm, is where the yield case actually lives.

## Overview

Portofino is a yacht-harbour borgo of roughly 400 residents. Three things set its pricing: Parco di Portofino supply caps that make new stock effectively impossible, superyacht tourism calendars, and global name recognition. Together they put per-sqm values at the top of the Ligurian ladder and keep them there.

Treat harbour frontage as the primary value driver on €1,200,000-6,000,000 tickets where American and Swiss buyers dominate off-market enquiry pipelines.

1. Village arithmetic begins with scarcity you cannot escape on protected peninsula land.

3. Resale depth tracks global HNW discretionary cycles more than Italian mortgage market.

Harbour-facing borgo stock in Portofino trades at €12,000-18,000 per sqm; interior units without a marina sightline fall to €8,000-11,000, and headland villas blend €10,000-16,000 depending on privacy and access. Public portal averages above €11,000 per sqm in early 2026 understate the market, because the trophy transactions clear off-market.

## Rental Yields and Tenant Profiles

A licensed harbour-view two-bedroom in Portofino lets for €4,000-8,000 per week in August but fills only 12-16 weeks a year, which produces 2-3% gross on a ticket above €1,400,000. The guest pool is American, British and northern European, and it expects concierge-grade service.

Management at this level costs 20-30% of gross, so two identical €1,800,000 purchases can differ by more than a full percentage point of net return once IMU and cedolare secca at 21% are added.

| Strategy | Gross yield | Tenant type |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Harbour-view STR | 2.0-2.8% | Superyacht tourism |
| Headland weekly villa | 2.0-3.0% | Luxury charter guests |
| Interior borgo STR | 2.5-3.5% | Lower base, thinner premium |
| Long-term lease | 1.5-2.5% | Rare at top end |

1. Model net yield after IMU and cedolare secca at 21% before marketing peak-season STR.
2. Assume 12-16 occupied weeks unless verified booking data exists for exact harbour address.
3. Set yield math against [Santa Margherita Ligure](/areas/santa-margherita-ligure/) at 3-4% gross on lower entry.

## What risks should foreign buyers watch in Portofino?

Portofino's risks are concentrated rather than diffuse. Occupied weeks below twelve turn a 2-3% gross into something closer to nothing after management. Mooring rights advertised in listings are frequently informal arrangements rather than transferable concessions, and the marketing value evaporates when that is discovered. Interior stock without a harbour sightline carries an 18-30 month resale drag. Confirm the Parco di Portofino vincolo status and pull three years of administrator spese before the compromesso.

| Risk | Typical trigger | Mitigation |
|------|-----------------|------------|
| Park renovation delay | Soprintendenza queue | 12-24 month permit budget |
| Mooring misclaim | No transferable berth | Port authority verification |
| STR regulation | Condominium bans | Verify regolamento before deposit |
| Liquidity gap | Interior no-view unit | Buy harbour sightline |
| Climate maintenance | Salt air on fabric | 1-1.5% annual value budget |

1. Reject mooring marketing without port authority concession documentation attached to listing.
2. Attach Parco landscape vincolo report before planning terrace or pool capex on headland villas.
3. Model vacancy separately for November-March on identical €1,500,000 underwriting sheets.

## What should foreign buyers verify before Portofino rogito?

Four items decide a Portofino purchase at €1,200,000-4,000,000: the Parco di Portofino vincolo status and what it permits, whether any mooring right is a registered concession or an informal arrangement, three years of administrator spese with approved works, and whether the cadastral plan matches the built layout after decades of internal alterations.

Confirm CIN transferability for short-let marketing and the IMU band before authorising a wire from a non-resident account.

| Document | Why it matters | Red flag |
|----------|----------------|----------|
| Park vincolo report | Renovation scope limits | Agent omits landscape status |
| Mooring concession | Berth marketing claims | Dock without legal title |
| Spese history (3 yr) | Net yield after €300/mo condo | Agent omits from packet |
| Cadastral plan | Room count vs registry | Terrace not registered |
| CIN registration | STR compliance | Pro forma without transfer |

1. Request park vincolo and mooring concession documentation before compromesso on harbour-marketed stock.
2. Confirm architect familiarity with Parco bureaucracy before planning any interior renovation scope.
3. Cross-read [Liguria guide](/guides/liguria-property-investment-guide/) and [Italy rental yield guide](/guides/italy-rental-yield-guide/).
4. Work IMU and cedolare secca at 21% through with a commercialista before the compromesso wire.
5. Order independent avvocato review on seller declarations and conformità gaps on pre-1960 palazzi.
6. Verify CIN transferability before peak-season STR pro forma on harbour tickets below €2,500,000.
