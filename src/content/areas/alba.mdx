## Overview

Alba is the working capital of the Langhe: the town where the Barolo and Barbaresco trade is administered, where the white truffle fair runs each autumn, and where Ferrero has its headquarters. That mix gives it something most wine-country addresses lack: domestic employment that fills leases from November to March, when remote [Langhe](/areas/langhe/) cascine sit empty.

Stock inside the old wall ring resells faster than countryside property when priced to the OMI band on €280,000-350,000 tickets.

## Rental Yields and Tenant Profiles

Furnished long leases in central Alba to wine-sector executives and hospitality managers return 3-4.5% gross on tickets under €350,000, and tenants frequently sign remotely from Milan and Turin employers. A deeded parking space is close to mandatory at that end of the market. Running the same unit as a licensed short-let around the truffle weekends shifts the net figure by 100-150 basis points once IMU and cedolare secca at 21% are applied.

Weighed as pros and cons, Alba gives a walkable wine capital with winter tenants and Ferrero payroll behind it, against modest yields and a foreign resale pool far thinner than Tuscany's. Three Piedmontese options, three different problems. Alba at €2,200-2,300 per sqm gives wine-country identity with a walkable town and no car dependency.

Turin's Politecnico corridors give deeper year-round tenant demand and no truffle-season concentration. Remote Langhe cascine give the views and the land but the thinnest winter market of the three. ## How does Alba connect to Milan and Turin?

Alba is 50-70 minutes from Turin by car and 60-90 minutes from Milan, which makes a split week between a northern office and a Langhe base realistic on €280,000-350,000 tickets. Barolo village is 20-30 minutes from the centro, so a dual-track Piedmont allocation (town apartment plus vineyard-view holiday unit) is geographically practical. Rail exists but is slow; buyers here should assume car dependency.

| Destination | Approximate time | Investor use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turin centro | 50-70 min car | Commuter furnished lease |
| Milan centro | 60-90 min car | Weekend wine-country base |
| Barolo village | 20-30 min car | Hill-town day trips |
| Langhe cascine | 15-40 min car | Agriturismo oversight |

1. A33 and SS231 corridors support Milan-Turin split-week commuter strategies.
2. Car dependency is mandatory for hill village access without expecting daily rail from remote cascine.
3. Barolo adjacency enables urban services plus vineyard exposure within one province budget.

## What risks should foreign buyers watch in Alba?

Three risks. Parking scarcity, which executive tenants reject outright and which no renovation can fix; short-let regulation in the centro, which moves net returns without notice; and a 12-18 month resale drag on lifestyle stock mispriced 8-12% above its OMI quartiere band. Red flag checklist requires CIN verification and three-year administrator spese packets before compromesso on €250,000-350,000 urban tickets for foreign investors.

| Centro conformità | Palazzo conversion gaps | Engineer inspection pre-offer |

3. Model vacancy separately for non-truffle months on STR-heavy underwriting spreadsheets.

## What should foreign buyers verify before Alba rogito?

On €250,000-350,000 Langhe capital stock, confirm four things before the compromesso: that the parking deed transfers, three years of administrator spese with approved works, that the cadastral category matches actual use, and the CIN path if short-let around the truffle fair is planned. Alba's IMU on second homes falls in the 0.76-0.86% band on cadastral value; confirm the current rate before authorising a wire from a non-resident account.

| Document | Why it matters | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parking deed | Executive tenant conversion | Shared courtyard only |
| Spese history (3 yr) | Net yield after €150/mo condo | Agent omits from packet |
| Conformità edilizia | Palazzo conversion legality | Interior layout mismatch |
| Cadastral category | IMU and registration tax | A/2 vs C/1 mismatch |
| CIN registration | Truffle STR compliance | Pro forma without transfer |

1. Request parking deed and three-year condominium spese history before compromesso.
2. Cross-read [Piedmont guide](/guides/piedmont-property-investment-guide/) and [Langhe area guide](/areas/langhe/).
3. Model IMU and cedolare secca at 21 percent with commercialista before compromesso wire.
4. Order independent avvocato review on seller declarations and conformità gaps on pre-1980 stock.
5. Verify CIN transferability before truffle-weekend STR pro forma on centro tickets below €320,000.
6. Align steps with [Italy rental yield guide](/guides/italy-rental-yield-guide/) net modeling framework.
