## Overview

Polignano a Mare is a comune of about 17,000 people built on a limestone cliff above the Adriatic, with Lama Monachile (the cove beach in every photograph of Puglia) at its centre. Developable cliff inventory is close to fixed, and seasonality is sharper than in Bari or the Ostuni white-city corridors.

German and British buyers make up roughly 30-35% of foreign enquiry on €350,000-650,000 cliff tickets, and they buy for short-let and lifestyle, not for long leases.

## Rental Yields and STR Economics

A CIN-compliant bilocale with parking at €350,000-650,000 delivers 5-8% gross under professional management. The arithmetic runs on 65-75% annual occupancy blending €200-400 peak nightly rates against €80-120 shoulder-season floors. German and British guests dominate the booking profile, which shapes what the apartment needs to look like.

Net cash flow after IMU, platform fees, cleaning, and cedolare secca at 21% first property or 26% second because gross STR screenshots from July weeks overstate annual returns by 200-300 basis points.

Polignano's resale market is unusually narrow for a place with such wide recognition, and the reason is physical. The cliff stock that carries the brand is a small number of properties, most of them without parking, reached through pedestrian streets, so the buyers who want them are a specific group willing to accept those constraints for the address. That produces two very different outcomes on the same street. A cliff property with a documented CIN and either a parking deed or a resident permit, priced against closed comparables, clears to that group within a normal season. A cliff property without them competes against every other Puglian coastal listing on price alone. On a €350,000-650,000 hold, the resale case is settled by the compliance file assembled at purchase, and German and British buyers at 30-35% of enquiry are the pool that eventually reads it.
