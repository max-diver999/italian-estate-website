### Historic Centre (Duomo, Broletto, walled town)

The Gothic-Romanesque Duomo and Broletto define Como's civic identity. Apartments range from medieval cores with low ceilings to renovated units with lift access. Price range €5,000-9,000/m²; typical two-bedroom €400k-750k after renovation. Nightly STR rates €120-220 in peak season for quality two-bed units; shoulder seasons matter because Lake Como is not August-only, but winter occupancy drops sharply. Challenges include condominium rules restricting sub-30-day lets, scarce parking, and humidity requiring seismic and moisture surveys.

### Lungo Lario and Lakefront Promenade

The waterfront strip from Piazza Cavour toward the public gardens commands premium views and highest foot traffic. Price range €6,000-12,000/m²; lake-view two-bedroom €550k-1.1M. STR-forward with international guests paying for promenade access. Expect management intensity, turnover, cleaning, and guest communication in multiple languages. Noise from events and weekend crowds; strict building envelopes on façades.

### San Giovanni and Station Quarter

Como San Giovanni is the main rail link to Milan. Price range €4,000-7,000/m²; entry two-bedroom €320k-520k. Long-term dominant with Milan commuters, hospital and university staff, and young professionals on 12-36 month leases. Properties within 10 minutes walk of San Giovanni station command renter premia of 10-15% over hillside equivalents without parking.

### Brunate and Hillside (Funicular Como-Brunate)

Brunate sits above the city with panoramic lake and alpine views. Price range €4,500-8,000/m²; villas and larger apartments €450k-900k. Lifestyle STR and seasonal long-term; winter maintenance and access roads require budgeting on steep slopes with fewer daily services than city centre.

### Hinterland and Suburban Como (Camnago, Monte Olimpino)

Suburban districts offer parking, modern builds, and family tenants. Price range €3,000-6,000/m²; modern three-bedroom €280k-480k. Long-term families, Milan commuters with cars, remote workers. Among the best pure rent math in the Como metro, though less international resale appeal than iconic addresses.

## What short-term rental rules apply in Como?

Three registrations are required before a listing goes live: a national CIN, Lombardy regional registration, and Como comune compliance including guest registers. Income is taxed at 21% cedolare secca on up to two properties and 26% beyond that — a threshold that catches owners building a small portfolio on one lake. Red flag checklist requires regolamento di condominio review because building bylaws can ban sub-30-day lets even when the comune permits STR on €550,000-1.1M lakefront tickets for foreign buyers.

| Layer | Requirement | Cost / penalty signal |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| National CIN | Ministry of Tourism portal | Fines + platform delisting without code |
| Lombardy regional | Hospitality alignment | Density considerations apply |
| Como comune | Guest registry, tourism tax | TARI classification tied to occupancy |
| Condominium | Regolamento review | Silent agent on building bans |
| Cedolare secca | 21% up to 2 properties; 26% beyond | vs IRPEF scaling for high earners |

1. Obtain CIN through municipal authorization before marketing STR income on portal pro forma.
2. Read regolamento di condominio before purchase on historic waterfront buildings.
3. Budget €800-2,000 initial registration plus €1,500-3,000/year fiscal representative for non-residents.
