**Quick answer:** In Chianti the land is the least complicated part of the purchase. What determines value is a set of registered entitlements: the planting authorisation, the vineyard register entry, and the claimable surface for the denomination. None of those behaves like a title deed, and one of them cannot be transferred to another company at all.

The Chianti purchase that goes wrong is rarely the one where the roof leaks. It is the one where a buyer paid a vineyard price for land whose entitlements did not come with it, and discovered the gap after the deed. The instruments involved are agricultural rather than property law, they are administered by the region rather than by the comune, and a conveyancer who has only ever handled apartments will not think to ask about them.

## What is actually being sold when a farmhouse comes with vines?

Four distinct things, usually quoted as one price.

| Item | Nature | Where it is recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land and buildings | Real property | Catasto and conservatoria |
| Planted vineyard | Part of the land | Vineyard register |
| Planting authorisation | Administrative entitlement | Regional authorisation system |
| Claimable denomination area | Administrative entitlement | Vineyard register, per denomination |

The first two move with the deed in the ordinary way. The second two are where the money is, and they follow their own rules. A seller can be entirely honest and still hand over less than the buyer believed, because the buyer was reading a property transaction and the seller was selling an agricultural holding.

## What replaced planting rights in 2016?

Regulation (EU) 1308/2013 abolished the old transferable planting rights and put an authorisation scheme in their place. The features that matter to a purchaser are specific and checkable.

- An authorisation corresponds to a stated area expressed in hectares, not to a general permission to plant
- It is valid for three years from the date of grant
- A producer who does not use an authorisation within its validity is exposed to administrative penalties set by the member state
- New planting authorisations are made available annually up to 1% of the national planted area declared at 31 July of the year before the applications
- Grubbing up an existing vineyard generates a replanting authorisation, which is the ordinary route by which an established holding keeps its area

The transfer rule is the one that catches foreign buyers. An authorisation issued for an area equivalent to a grubbed-up vineyard is not transferable to another undertaking and may only be used within the holding that holds it, except in cases such as succession. Since the 2017 and 2018 campaigns, new planting authorisations have also ceased to be transferable from one region to another.

Read that against a typical purchase structure. If the seller is a company and the buyer takes the land rather than the company, an authorisation sitting with the seller's undertaking does not follow the parcels. Whether the deal is a share purchase or an asset purchase stops being a tax question and becomes the question that determines what the buyer receives.

## Which parcels can carry the denomination at all?

Chianti Classico's production rules do not simply describe a geographic area; they set physical qualifying conditions for entry in the vineyard register. Only hillside vineyards with suitable orientation qualify, on soils predominantly composed of sandstone, calcareous marl, shale, sand and gravel, situated at an altitude no higher than 700 metres above sea level.

That has an immediate consequence for anyone comparing two estates on price per hectare. Land in the geographic zone that fails the position, orientation or altitude tests is agricultural land with a view. It is not potential denomination land waiting for someone with ambition, and no amount of investment converts it.

The register then draws a second distinction, between the vineyard physically planted and the surface that may be claimed for a denomination. The claimable surface is what governs how much wine can lawfully be sold under the name, and it is recorded per parcel. Two neighbouring estates of the same planted hectarage can therefore carry materially different commercial capacity, and the difference is invisible from the road.

## What do the production rules cap?

The Chianti Classico rules bind the output as well as the input. The wine is made from a minimum of 80% Sangiovese, with the balance from red varieties admitted to the register. Maximum grape yield is 75 quintals per hectare, corresponding to 52.50 hectolitres of wine, and the conversion of grapes to finished wine may not exceed 70%.

Those are among the most restrictive limits of any Italian denomination, and they are a valuation input rather than a technical footnote. A hectare's lawful revenue ceiling is fixed by the rules, so the variables a buyer can actually influence are price per bottle and cost per hectare. An underwriting model that assumes volume growth on existing hectares is modelling something the rules forbid.

## What does agricultural pre-emption do to the transaction?

Italian law gives certain cultivators a right to step into a sale of agricultural land, and it applies to a large share of Chianti transactions.

Article 8 of law 590 of 26 May 1965 confers the right on a direct cultivator holding the land under a lease. Article 7 of law 817 of 14 August 1971 extends it to a direct cultivator who owns adjoining land, where no tenant, sharecropper or similar party is established on the land being sold. The seller must notify the proposed sale and its terms to the holder of the right, who has a defined short period to take the sale on those terms. A sale completed without proper notice, or on terms different from those notified, can be attacked by the holder against the purchaser rather than only against the seller.

Practical handling looks like this:

1. Establish at the earliest stage which parcels are agricultural and who farms them, since the answer is often not the seller
2. Identify the adjoining owners and whether any of them is a registered direct cultivator
3. Require the seller to serve the notices and to evidence the responses before the deposit becomes non-refundable
4. Treat any pressure to skip this because everyone is friendly as the clearest possible signal to slow down
5. Keep the evidence, because it is what protects the buyer's title afterwards

## How should the diligence file be built?

What the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-italy-property/) describes is necessary here and nowhere near sufficient. An estate needs an agricultural layer on top of it, and those documents come from the region and the consortium rather than from the comune.

| Additional document | What it proves |
| --- | --- |
| Vineyard register extract per parcel | Planted area, varieties, claimable surface |
| Authorisation records | What exists, its area, its expiry |
| Consortium membership and quota position | Whether the holding can market under the name |
| Cultivation and lease agreements | Who holds pre-emption |
| Certificato di destinazione urbanistica | Whether the buildings can become what you want |

That last row is worth a sentence of its own. Converting agricultural buildings into accommodation is a planning question governed by the regional and municipal instruments, and in a landscape under heritage protection the answer is frequently narrower than a buyer expects. The [renovation cost guide](/guides/italy-property-renovation-costs-guide/) covers what the work costs once permitted, which is a different question from whether it will be.

## Asset purchase or share purchase?

Because the authorisation is tied to the undertaking rather than to the parcels, the structure of the deal decides what changes hands. This is the point at which a Chianti transaction stops resembling a house purchase.

| Structure | What moves | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset purchase | Land, buildings, and the register position attaching to the parcels | Authorisations held by the seller's undertaking may not follow |
| Share purchase | The undertaking with everything it holds | Every liability of the company comes too, including historic ones |
| Mixed | Negotiated split of assets and rights | Requires the agricultural and corporate advisers to be in the same room |

Neither route is generally correct. A share purchase preserves the entitlements and inherits the company's tax, employment and environmental history. An asset purchase leaves the history behind and may leave the entitlements with it. What is not defensible is choosing between them on tax advice alone, without anyone having established what the undertaking holds that the parcels do not, since that inventory is the only thing that makes the two structures comparable at all.

Two practical consequences follow. Warranties in a Chianti sale should address the register position and the authorisations by name rather than being drafted from a residential template. And the timetable should assume that regional records take longer to obtain than municipal ones, which means starting the agricultural enquiries before the property enquiries rather than alongside them.

## What if the buyer only wants a house?

Many do, and it remains a rational purchase. The point is to buy it as what it is.

- Land that will not be farmed is still agricultural land for pre-emption and for taxation, and detaching it is a structuring decision taken before an offer
- A vineyard left unfarmed can lose its register position over time, which is a value loss rather than a saving
- Buyers who want the landscape without the entitlements should compare the hill towns of [Siena](/areas/siena/) and the [Val d'Orcia](/areas/val-dorcia/), where the equivalent estate carries fewer agricultural attachments
- Acquisition costs on agricultural land differ from those on a dwelling, and the [registration tax guide](/guides/italy-registration-tax-property/) sets out the residential position that does not automatically apply here
- On exit, the same instruments apply in reverse, which the [selling guide](/guides/selling-property-italy-foreigner/) frames for a foreign vendor

The wider Tuscan market is covered in the [Tuscany guide](/guides/tuscany-property-investment-guide/). Chianti's distinction within it is not that the houses are prettier or the views better. It is that the value sits in a set of registrations that a property lawyer will not look at unless asked, and that the most valuable of them cannot be sold to another company at all.
