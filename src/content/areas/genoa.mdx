## What geotechnical and heritage risks affect Genoa buyers?

Genoa is built on steep ground, and hillside stock needs a geotechnical survey before any renovation is costed: landslide vincoli can block terrace expansions that legacy agency brochures still advertise on €350,000 Carignano tickets. In the UNESCO-listed centro, facade changes require Soprintendenza coordination, which suburban condominiums avoid entirely — there the binding checks are elevator conformity and parking instead.

| Risk | Typical trigger | Mitigation |
|------|-----------------|------------|
| Landslide vincolo | Hillside terrace capex | Geotechnical engineer report |
| UNESCO facade rules | Centro exterior work | Soprintendenza filing |
| Elevator gap | Pre-1980 walk-up | Conformity certificate |
| Port noise | Dense historic fabric | Acoustic audit before lease marketing |
| Condominium spese spike | Post-war towers | 3-year administrator history |

1. Order geotechnical survey on hillside purchases before renovation budget lock.
2. Verify elevator certificates and pending extraordinary works on post-war towers.
3. Budget acoustic engineer input before marketing furnished leases near Via Garibaldi corridors.

## What should foreign buyers verify before Genoa rogito?

On €260,000-420,000 urban tickets, four items precede the compromesso: geotechnical survey results on any hillside position, the elevator conformity certificate, confirmation that the parking deed transfers, and three years of administrator spese with approved works. On UNESCO centro palazzi, check that the cadastral plan matches the built layout, and confirm the IMU band before authorising a wire from a non-resident account.
