## What Is the Campobasso vs Termoli Investment Choice?
On a €170,000 budget, inland Campobasso at about €1,085 per sqm buys nearly twice the floor area of Adriatic Termoli at about €1,861, and a completely different income profile. Campobasso lets year-round to hospital and university tenants; Termoli earns most of its money between April and October. The two are 45 minutes apart and are the only real options in Molise.

The key decision: choose Campobasso for year-round urban yield without coastal seasonality void compressing November to March cash flow. Choose Termoli for Adriatic beach tourism narrative, summer rental spikes April through October, and higher monthly rent bands accepting higher acquisition basis and CIN licensing complexity.

Molise splits inland and coastal at roughly the same distance apart as an hour's drive. Campobasso centro runs near €1,085 per sqm; Termoli's lido-adjacent stock starts at €165,000 for a two-bedroom and prices at a premium for the beach. The gap is around 20% per sqm.

## What Rental Yields Can Each City Deliver?

Termoli produces the higher headline yield and Campobasso the steadier one, and the difference is seasonality rather than rent level. Foreign buyers in Termoli earn most of the year in a 3 to 4 month summer, while Campobasso's public-sector and hospital tenants sign 12-month contracts.

## Which City Offers Better Resale Liquidity for Foreign Buyers?

Neither is liquid by national standards, but they fail differently: Campobasso resells to a domestic buyer in 6-12 months when priced to the OMI band, while Termoli's coastal stock draws a wider summer audience and a thinner winter one, extending sale cycles to 12-18 months. The gross figure means little until IMU, cedolare secca, spese and void weeks have been deducted; get a commercialista to run that calculation before wiring.

1. Track three OMI-quartiere closed sales in each city before pricing Campobasso versus Termoli exit tickets.
2. Model time-on-market at 6-18 months depending on foreign enquiry depth at 10-15% closing costs reference bands.
3. Confirm parking deeds and conformità documentation on portal listings before marketing to Anglo-American buyers.

Termoli coast branding supports summer tourism marketing in resale copy. Campobasso wins on absolute ticket size for buyers prioritizing capital deployment efficiency over beach narrative.

## What Risks Should Molise Buyers Watch in Each City?

Both cities sit in an active seismic zone, so earthquake compliance on older stock is the shared risk. Campobasso's specific exposure is student turnover on the academic calendar; Termoli's is short-stay regulation and a November-to-March occupancy gap that compresses annual cash flow. Both carry special-assessment risk on pre-1990 towers near the hospital and lungomare corridors, and both price spring listings 8-12% above winter closings.

- **Campobasso:** Thinner foreign resale, public sector wage sensitivity
- **Termoli:** STR licensing, summer concentration, higher per sqm basis
- **Both:** Earthquake conformity, portal asking versus closed sale gaps

## What Should Foreign Buyers Verify Before Choosing?

Both towns share one gating check and differ on a second. Earthquake-zone compliance on pre-1980 stock applies in each. Beyond that, Campobasso needs a parking deed for hospital tenants, while Termoli needs a transferable CIN — without which the summer season, which is the whole coastal case, cannot be let.

### Why the Two Markets Behave Differently

The comparison is really between two demand structures rather than two price levels. Campobasso is an administrative and university town: its tenant base is salaried public-sector staff, hospital employees and students, which produces low headline yields but twelve-month occupancy and rent that moves slowly in either direction. Termoli is a seasonal coastal market, where a large share of the annual return is earned in a few summer weeks and the rest of the year carries the cost of an empty flat.

That difference decides which risks matter. In Campobasso the binding question is resale: the foreign buyer pool is thin, so an exit depends on domestic demand and can take considerably longer than in a coastal comune. In Termoli the binding question is regulatory, because the summer income the price assumes is only lawful with a CIN registration and a condominium that permits tourist use. Confirm both before the caparra rather than after, since neither can be fixed by renegotiating the price.
