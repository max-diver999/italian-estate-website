## How do Puglia vs Tuscany compare at a glance?

Puglia returns 5-8% gross short-let on tickets of €80,000-400,000; Tuscany returns 4-7% on €200,000-800,000. German buyers take 35-40% of southern enquiry, Americans dominate the Tuscan pool. On identical €250,000-400,000 capital the decision is not really about yield: it is about whether you want the higher income now or the deeper, faster resale market later, because Tuscany has consistently offered the second and Puglia the first.

| Metric | Puglia | Tuscany |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Gross yield band | 5-8% STR | 4-7% mixed |
| Entry renovation | €80k-150k | €200k-400k |
| Restored mid-market | €150k-400k | €400k-800k |
| Capital appreciation | 4-12% volatile | 3-5% steady |
| Occupancy STR | 60-75% | 70-85% |
| Foreign liquidity | Developing | Established |
| Top foreign buyer | Germany 35-40% | US 30-35% |

1. Choose Puglia for yield optimization and authentic trulli or coastal STR on sub-€400,000 tickets.
2. Choose Tuscany for capital appreciation, wine country lifestyle, and faster foreign resale above €400,000.
3. Read [best regions 2026 guide](/guides/best-regions-invest-italy-property-2026/) before southern-northern allocation.

## How do entry prices compare in Puglia versus Tuscany?

Puglia renovation projects start at €80,000-150,000 against €200,000-400,000 in Tuscany on 2026 portal data; prime Ostuni trulli run €150,000-350,000 while comparable Chianti stock sits at €400,000-600,000. The gap is roughly two to one across every tier. Spring asking prices overshoot winter closings by 10-15% on €250,000 tickets in both regions, so take three closed sales per micro-market before making an offer.

| Ticket type | Puglia indicative | Tuscany indicative |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Value renovation | €80k-150k inland | €200k-350k secondary |
| Coastal / trulli mid | €150k-350k | n/a direct compare |
| Chianti / Val d'Orcia | n/a | €400k-800k restored |
| Restoration capex | €30k-80k typical | €50k-150k typical |
| Closing cost band | 7-12% | 8-13% |

1. A €250,000 budget buys restored Ostuni trulli or secondary Tuscany farmhouse needing selective renovation.
2. Model trulli restoration at €800-1,500 per sqm before comparing total project cost across regions.
3. Cross-read [Valle d'Itria area](/areas/valle-d-itria/) and [Siena area](/areas/siena/) for micro-market depth.

## How do rental yields compare in Puglia versus Tuscany?

Puglia short-let delivers 5-8% gross, with Ostuni's peak summer weeks pushing the better-bought stock to the top of that range. Tuscany's mixed income model reaches 4-7%, with Chianti farmhouses at 4-6% but a far longer shoulder season. Model the net after cedolare secca: 21% on a long lease, 26% on a second short-let unit, because Puglia's November-to-April void frequently reverses the headline gap entirely.

| Strategy | Puglia gross | Tuscany gross | Best region |
|----------|--------------|---------------|-------------|
| Coastal STR | 6-8% peak | 4-6% coast | Puglia yield |
| Trulli cultural STR | 5-7% annual | n/a | Puglia unique |
| Chianti farmhouse STR | n/a | 4-6% | Tuscany brand |
| Academic long lease | 3-4% Lecce | 3-3.5% Florence | Tuscany stability |
| Luxury villa STR | 5-7% Salento | 5-7% Val d'Orcia | Comparable peak |

### Puglia STR and trulli income economics

Take a restored trullo bought at €220,000 producing €13,600 of annual short-let gross at 65% occupancy across a May-to-October season: that is 6.2% before IMU and before cedolare secca at 26% on a second unit. German guests, at 35-40% of enquiry, drive most of the booking conversion on [Valle d'Itria](/areas/valle-d-itria/) stock. The figure holds only while occupancy does, and occupancy here is a six-month proposition.

1. Model November-April void honestly before Puglia coast-only STR pro forma on €200,000 tickets.
2. Verify CIN registration and regional codice identificativo before first booking on trulli inventory.
3. Compare net yield against Tuscany Chianti farmhouse at 4-5% gross on €450,000 restored ticket.

### Tuscany wine country and urban income economics

A Chianti farmhouse at €480,000 producing €28,000 of annual short-let gross at 75% occupancy returns 5.8% before IMU: a lower headline than Puglia, on a longer season. Tuscany's winter shoulder demand, driven by culture and city breaks rather than beaches, is what closes the gap on the annual number. American lifestyle buyers account for 30-35% of enquiry, and they buy for the hold rather than the yield.

1. Budget €50,000-150,000 restoration on Tuscany farmhouses versus €30,000-80,000 on comparable Puglia stock.
2. Confirm municipal STR day limits in Florence UNESCO zones before urban STR pro forma on €400,000+ tickets.
3. Default to long lease September-May in Florence when UNESCO STR license path uncertain.

## How do STR regulations compare in Puglia versus Tuscany?

The regulatory gap is now wide and moving further apart. Puglia keeps a relatively liberal position: CIN registration and tourist tax of €1-3 per person per night. Tuscany layers municipal limits on top, including Florence's ban on new historic-centre licences, extended city-wide in May 2026, and 120-180 day caps in several comuni. Red flag checklist flags agents marketing Florence centro STR.

| Factor | Puglia | Tuscany |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Licensing difficulty | Low-moderate | Moderate-high |
| Annual rental day limits | Generally unlimited | Often 120-180 days |
| Tourist tax | €1-3/night | €2-5/night |
| Florence UNESCO | n/a | New license bans |
| Enforcement level | Low-moderate | Moderate-high |
| Registration timeline | 30-60 days | 30-90 days |

1. Confirm CIN and municipal STR path before Puglia summer pro forma on coastal tickets from €150,000.
2. Verify exact Florence address against UNESCO STR policy before deposit on Oltrarno inventory.
3. Cross-read [Italy holiday let licensing](/guides/italy-holiday-let-licensing/) before STR allocation in either region.

That difference compounds: stress-test a seven-year exit with 3-5% agent commission and IMU paid throughout the hold before treating a €80,000 Puglian entry as the cheaper of the two.

## What risks should foreign buyers watch in Puglia and Tuscany?

Five risks apply in both regions and trigger differently in each. Capex overruns start with trullo vault damage in Puglia and farmhouse structure in Tuscany; seasonal void with a winter short-let pro forma against a shoulder-heavy mix; compliance with a missing CIN against Florence's licence ban. Budget 15-20% restoration contingency on Puglian trulli before underwriting anything.

| Risk | Puglia trigger | Tuscany trigger |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| Capex overrun | Trulli vault damage | Farmhouse structural |
| Seasonal void | Winter STR pro forma | Shoulder vs peak mix |
| STR compliance | Missing CIN | Florence license ban |
| Liquidity drag | Inland secondary | Overpriced Chianti |
| Regulatory change | Evolving Puglia policy | UNESCO enforcement |

1. Budget 15-20% restoration contingency on Puglia trulli before underwriting net yield on €200,000 total project.
2. Confirm Florence STR license on exact address before deposit on €400,000+ urban tickets.
3. Model vacancy separately for Puglia November-April and Tuscany academic calendar on identical €300,000 tickets.

In Tuscany: the Soprintendenza filing requirement on any planned centro renovation. In both: the CIN registration path, the IMU cadastral band, and whether the cedolare secca election will be 21% on a long lease or 26% on a second short-let unit.
