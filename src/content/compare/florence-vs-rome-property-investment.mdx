## What Is the Florence vs Rome Property Investment Snapshot for 2026?

Florence sells Renaissance prestige at a €4,737 per sqm city average with foreign buyers above 30% of central deals. Rome sells capital-city depth at €3,500-6,500 per sqm on prime stock, with Jubilee-year tourism running through 2026 and a resale market several times larger. Typical underwriting models 2.8-4.5% long-term gross in Florence permitted zones, 2.5-5% mixed Rome strategies.

## How Do Florence and Rome Property Prices Compare Per Square Metre?

Florence is typically the tighter market per sqm and Rome the more varied, which is a supply story rather than a demand one. Florence's UNESCO centro caps what can be built or converted, while Rome's scale means foreign buyers meet a far wider spread between central and peripheral quarters.

| Zone | Florence €/m² | Rome €/m² | Investor read |
|------|---------------|-----------|---------------|
| UNESCO historic core | €6,000-8,000 | €4,500-6,500 (Municipio I) | Florence premium on comparable heritage |
| STR-friendly fringe | €4,200-5,500 (Campo di Marte) | €4,000-5,500 (Trastevere fringe) | Rome offers more compliant STR paths |
| Value regeneration | €3,200-4,000 (Novoli) | €2,800-4,200 (Ostiense, EUR) | Rome lower entry on modern stock |
| Trophy address | €8,000+ (Arno-facing) | €6,500+ (Piazza-facing) | Both thin markets, long marketing |

Peripheral Florence quartieri including Isolotto and Gavinana offer €2,800-3,600/m² with yields reaching 4.5-5.2% gross on long-term leases but slower foreign lifestyle resale.

## Which City Offers Higher Rental Yields and Income Strategies?

Rome yields more and Florence exits better. Roman prime districts at €3,500-6,500 per sqm return 2.5-5% across embassy, academic and short-let demand. Florence at about €4,737 returns 2.5-4%, and its short-let route has been closed city-wide since May 2026 — which pushes owners onto long leases the price basis does not support.

| Strategy | Florence | Rome |
|----------|----------|------|
| Long-term furnished | 2.8-4.5% gross | 2.5-3.5% gross |
| Licensed STR | 3-4% permitted zones | 3.5-5% Trastevere/Prati |
| Student/academic | Oltrarno, Campo di Marte | San Lorenzo, Trastevere |
| Corporate/diplomatic | Limited vs Rome | Strong Municipio I demand |

Neither city is a pure yield market like southern Italy value corridors. Model net after IMU, 21-26% cedolare secca, and void months honestly on both markets.

## How Do UNESCO and Heritage Restrictions Compare?

Both cities restrict what a foreign buyer may do, but they restrict different things. Florence bans new short-let in its dense UNESCO zones outright; Rome applies density scrutiny with more available paths. Renovation runs €1,500-2,500 per sqm in Florence against €1,400-2,400 in Rome, and compliant short-let yields 3-4% against 3.5-5% in Trastevere and Prati.

| Rule | Florence | Rome |
|------|----------|------|
| New STR in centro | Banned in dense UNESCO zones | Density scrutiny, more paths |
| Renovation capex | €1,500-2,500/m² | €1,400-2,400/m² |
| Gross STR (compliant) | 3-4% permitted zones | 3.5-5% Trastevere/Prati |
| Long-term gross | 2.8-3.8% | 2.5-3.5% |
| Closing costs | 10-15% second home | 10-15% second home |

Florence investors cannot assume tourist use on fresh centro purchases inside the buffer. Rome investors face abusi edilizi risk on heritage units with conformità gaps adding legal costs. ## How Do Art Tourism and Jubilee Demand Differ for Rental Income?

Florence's tourism demand is steady and year-round; Rome's carries an event overlay that fades once the event does. Foreign buyers should treat Jubilee-driven projections as a temporary uplift rather than a new baseline, and underwrite the ordinary year that follows it instead.

1. Confirm visura catastale and conformità edilizia before compromesso on Florence versus Rome tickets.
2. Model net yield after IMU and 21% cedolare secca with commercialista at 30% gross assumptions.
3. Cross-read area guides and national tax frameworks before wire authorization to notaio escrow.

Florence's demand is deeper per capita in the historic centre during peak art season. Rome's demand base is broader across employment, diplomacy, and mass tourism. STR operators in Rome can diversify across Trastevere nightly tourism and Prati long-term corporate lets; Florence operators face tighter license scarcity in centro.

### What happens to Rome pricing after the Jubilee?

Event-driven demand recedes, and the useful question for a foreign buyer is what the ordinary year underneath it looks like. Florence's visitor demand is steady and largely independent of any single event, while Rome's Jubilee uplift is a temporary overlay on a market that is already large and varied.

| Horizon | Florence | Rome |
|---|---|---|
| During the event | Steady, unaffected | Elevated across centro districts |
| The year after | Unchanged | Reverts toward the underlying trend |
| What to underwrite | The ordinary year | The ordinary year, not the peak |

- The 10% to 15% closing stack and the 5-year capital gains window apply in both cities regardless.
- In Rome, the condominium regolamento remains the constraint that most often removes a letting case.

## What Are the Pros and Cons of Florence vs Rome?

Florence offers a tighter supply story and a deeper international resale pool, while Rome offers scale, variety and a far wider spread between central and peripheral quarters. Each carries its own principal risk: restricted licensing in one city, and condominium prohibitions on tourist use in the other.

| City | Advantage | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Florence | UNESCO tourism, strong STR | Soprintendenza compliance cost |
| Rome | Corporate tenant depth, scale | Centro pricing and bureaucracy |

- Florence: restricted short-let licensing inside the UNESCO centro removes many tourism cases.
- Rome: the condominium regolamento can forbid tourist use regardless of the comune's position.
- Both: 10% to 15% all-in entry, and capital gains taxable on a sale within 5 years.
