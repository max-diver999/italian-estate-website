## How do cedolare secca and IRPEF compare for landlords?

Cedolare secca taxes rent received at a flat 21% on a standard long lease, 10% on an eligible canone concordato and 26% from the second short-let unit. Ordinary IRPEF applies progressive bands of 23% to 43%, plus regional and municipal surcharges, to 95% of the rent — the 5% forfait is the only reduction a private landlord gets. Neither regime allows a deduction for IMU, maintenance, management or mortgage interest, so the election turns on your total income and your marginal band, not on how expensive the property is to run.

| Factor | Cedolare secca | Ordinary IRPEF |
|---|---|---|
| Tax base | Rent collected | 95% of rent collected (5% forfait) |
| Standard long-term rate | 21% | 23% to 43% progressive + regional add-ons |
| Subsidised concordato rate | 10% (eligible municipalities) | IRPEF brackets on 66.5% of rent |
| First STR property | 21% on gross bookings | 23% to 43% on gross bookings |
| Second STR unit | 26% on gross | 23% to 43% on gross bookings |
| IMU deductible | No | No — not against residential rental income |
| Maintenance, management, interest | Not deductible | Not deductible |
| Filing complexity | Lower: F24 from registered lease | Higher: Modello Redditi PF, more records |
| Best for | Almost all landlords with other income | Low total income, or absorbing renovation detrazioni |
| Non-resident eligible | Yes, with codice fiscale | Yes |

## What Is Cedolare Secca on Italian Rental Income?

1. Request updated visura catastale before modeling IMU on non-primary Cedolare Secca versus Irpef stock.
2. Compare 21% of gross rent against your marginal IRPEF band applied to 95% of it, surcharges included.
3. Budget separate F24 payments for IMU and rental tax independent of registration tax paid at rogito.

The standard rate is 21% on free-market residential leases, including the first short-term tourist rental property. A reduced 10% rate applies only to concordato leases (typically 3+2 years) in municipalities that run subsidized rent programs. Short-term income from a second, third, or fourth STR property is taxed at 26% on gross bookings under cedolare secca, not 21%.

Election is per contract at registration with the Agenzia delle Entrate. You pay through the F24 form, usually quarterly or annually depending on how your commercialista structures remittance. Platforms like Airbnb may withhold 21% at source for non-residents, but you remain responsible for topping up to 26% on additional units or correcting misclassified income.

Cedolare secca does not replace IMU, tourist taxes, or condominium charges. It covers income tax only. That separation matters when you model net yield: a Milan apartment grossing €24,000 may pay €5,040 cedolare secca plus €1,200 to €2,000 IMU plus management, with none of those costs reducing the flat tax.

## How Does IRPEF Tax Italian Rental Income?

IRPEF taxes rental income at progressive rates from 23% to 43%, applied to 95% of the rent received. The 5% forfait is the entire allowance: for an individual letting residential property, maintenance, management, condominium charges, insurance, IMU and mortgage interest are all non-deductible. This is the point most comparisons get wrong, and it changes the arithmetic completely — a high-cost property does not produce a lower IRPEF bill.

IRPEF (Imposta sul Reddito delle Persone Fisiche) is Italy's ordinary personal income tax. Residential rent received by an individual is reddito fondiario: the taxable base is the higher of the revalued cadastral rendita or 95% of the rent, the 5% standing for maintenance in the aggregate. The forfait rises to 25% for property in the Venice historic centre, Giudecca, Murano and Burano, and to 35% for buildings under a heritage vincolo. A canone concordato lease in a designated high-tension commune reduces the base by a further 30%, giving an effective 66.5% of rent. Short-let income under 30 days is taxed on the gross received with no forfait at all, and platform commission is not deductible under either regime.

2026 national brackets on Italian-source income:

| IRPEF bracket | Taxable income band | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| First bracket | Up to €28,000 | 23% |
| Second bracket | €28,001 to €50,000 | 35% |
| Third bracket | Over €50,000 | 43% |

Regional and municipal surcharges add roughly 1.2% to 3.3% depending on region of tax residence. A tax resident in Lombardy with €60,000 of Italian rental net income can exceed 45% all-in marginal rate. A non-resident taxed only on Italian property income still uses the same brackets but may not owe regional surcharges in the same way; confirm with a commercialista.

Work an example. On €18,000 of annual rent, cedolare secca costs 21% of €18,000, or €3,780. Under IRPEF the base is €17,100, and at the 23% bottom band that is €3,933 before regional and municipal surcharges of roughly 1.2% to 3.3%, which push it past €4,100. A €4,000 IMU bill and a €3,500 roof repair change neither figure. Cedolare secca wins here, and it wins by more as the marginal band rises.

## Non-Resident vs Resident: Who Pays What?

Both regimes are open to non-residents, which surprises many buyers. A non-resident may elect cedolare secca at 21% or 26% exactly as a resident can. The difference is what sits alongside it: a resident's worldwide income enters IRPEF at 23-43%, while a non-resident is assessed on Italian-source income only.

Italy taxes Italian-source rental income where the property sits. A UK or US owner who stays tax resident abroad still owes Italian tax on rent from a Florence flat. A buyer who registers anagrafe and becomes Italian tax resident includes that rent in worldwide filing, but the cedolare secca versus IRPEF choice on the lease itself follows the same registration rules.

| Profile | Italian rental tax choice | Worldwide income impact |
|---|---|---|
| Non-resident foreign owner | Cedolare secca or IRPEF on Italian rent | Home country may tax same rent; treaty FTC often applies |
| Italian tax resident | Same election per lease | Rent stacks with salary, dividends, other Italian income |
| Article 24-bis flat tax resident | Cedolare secca or IRPEF on Italian rent | Italian rent sits outside the foreign-income lump sum |
| EU second-home owner | Same as non-resident if not anagrafe registered | Depends on home country residency |

Non-residents need an Italian codice fiscale and registered lease (RLI telematic filing). Some appoint a rappresentante fiscale when platforms do not withhold correctly. Residents must also track IVIE/IVAFE on foreign assets, irrelevant to most non-residents but critical after relocation.

## How do the cedolare secca rates differ between short and long lets?

The flat rate is 21% on a qualifying long lease and 21% on a first short-let property, rising to 26% from the second short-let unit onward. From a third property the option disappears entirely: the activity becomes a business, requiring a Partita IVA and ordinary IRPEF treatment.

Short-term rental tax comparisons assume valid CIN registration, commune licensing, Alloggiati Web guest reporting, and 26% STR tax election modeled separately from 21% long-term cedolare secca paths.

| Rental type | Stay length | Cedolare secca | IRPEF alternative | Extra compliance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard residential lease | 4+4 or 3+2 years | 21% (10% concordato) | Net IRPEF | RLI registration |
| Locazione breve, first property | Under 30 days per booking | 21% on gross | Net IRPEF | CIN mandatory, Alloggiati guest registry |
| Locazione breve, 2nd to 4th property | Under 30 days | 26% each | Net IRPEF | CIN per unit, platform reporting |
| Five or more STR units | Under 30 days | Business regime (VAT, P.IVA) | Corporate or IRPEF | SCIA, VAT accounting |

From 1 January 2026, managing three or more short-term properties triggers mandatory Partita IVA business registration under Decree Law 145/2023 reforms. Cedolare secca flat rates no longer apply at scale; activity is reclassified as impresa. Budget VAT at 10% on tourist lettings where applicable, plus social charges, before comparing to a single-unit 21% model.

Long-term leases avoid CIN but may produce lower gross rent in tourist zones. Short-term boosts gross but adds cleaning, platform fees, and municipal tourist taxes (€2 to €9.50 per guest night in major cities). Tax choice should follow net cash flow, not headline ADR. ## When Does IRPEF Beat Cedolare Secca?

IRPEF wins in a narrower set of cases than most guides suggest, and none of them turn on how much the property costs to run. Because cedolare secca ring-fences the rent and taxes it at 21% regardless of your other income, the regime is at its worst when your total income is low enough that IRPEF would tax the rent lightly or not at all, and when you have IRPEF liability you need the rent to generate.

The two genuine cases are these. A landlord whose rent is close to their only Italian income can fall inside the no-tax area and personal detrazioni, paying little or no IRPEF where cedolare secca would still take 21% of the gross. And a landlord claiming a renovation detrazione — the 50% or 36% bonus spread over ten annual instalments — needs IRPEF liability to set it against; electing cedolare secca removes the rent from the IRPEF base and the instalment can go unused.

Scenario 1: retired resident, rent is nearly all the income. Rent €14,000, no salary or pension of consequence. IRPEF base €13,300; gross IRPEF at 23% is €3,059, but personal detrazioni and the no-tax area cut the actual bill sharply. Cedolare secca takes €2,940 with no allowances available against it. Run the return both ways before registering — this is the profile where IRPEF genuinely wins.

Scenario 2: renovation year. Rent €22,000 with €30,000 spent on qualifying works. The €30,000 does not reduce the rental base under either regime; it generates a detrazione claimed in ten annual instalments. Under IRPEF the rent produces roughly €4,807 of tax before surcharges, against which the instalment can be set. Under cedolare secca the €4,620 flat charge sits outside IRPEF and the instalment finds nothing to offset unless other income supplies it.

Scenario 3: salaried owner, simple lease. Rent €36,000 stacked on an existing salary already in the 35% band. Cedolare secca costs €7,560. Under IRPEF the €34,200 base is taxed at 35% and above, roughly €12,000 plus surcharges. Cedolare wins by a wide margin, which is why almost every landlord with other income elects it.

Scenario 4: canone concordato. A capped rent of €9,600 in a designated commune gives an IRPEF base of €6,384 after the 5% forfait and the 30% concordato reduction; at 23% that is €1,468 before surcharges. Cedolare secca at the concordato rate of 10% costs €960. The 10% rate is the lowest in the system and is very hard to beat.

Rule of thumb for 2026: if the rent is stacked on other income taxed at 35% or 43%, cedolare secca wins and the calculation is not close. If the rent is most of your Italian income, or you are carrying renovation detrazione instalments that need liability to absorb them, model both returns before registering. Do not choose IRPEF because the property is expensive to run — those costs reduce neither base.

## What Documentation Do You Need?

Electing cedolare secca requires the choice to be made per lease contract rather than per owner, and it must be communicated to the tenant in writing. Any non-resident buyer should hold the registered contract, the codice fiscale of every party, the visura catastale and F24 receipts before the first 21% payment falls due.

Keep the registered contract, every party's codice fiscale, the visura catastale and the F24 receipts together from the first payment onward, because the Agenzia delle Entrate reconciles them against platform data. Before the compromesso, confirm the conformità position and three years of condominium spese with an independent avvocato: those costs will not reduce your rental tax under either regime, so they belong in the yield model rather than the tax model.

| Step | Document / action | Who files | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Codice fiscale | Owner or consulate | Before purchase or lease |
| 2 | Ownership proof (rogito or visura catastale) | Notaio deed on file | At purchase |
| 3 | RLI lease registration | Landlord via Agenzia delle Entrate | Within 30 days of lease start |
| 4 | Cedolare secca election on RLI | Landlord | At registration |
| 5 | CIN (STR only) | Landlord via BDSR | Before first booking |
| 6 | Alloggiati Web guest reports (STR) | Landlord or manager | Within 24 hours of check-in |
| 7 | F24 tax payment | Commercialista | Quarterly or annual |
| 8 | Modello Redditi PF | Commercialista | If IRPEF path or mixed income |
| 9 | IMU declaration | Owner | Annual municipal filing |
| 10 | Platform withholding statements | Airbnb, Booking | Reconcile against F24 |

Keep scanned invoices for any renovation detrazione claim. Cedolare secca audits focus on under-reported gross rent, especially where platform data sharing to Agenzia delle Entrate begins under EU DAC7-style rules in May 2026. Match booking exports to registered RLI income.

## How Do CIN and STR Rules Affect Your Tax Choice?

CIN (Codice Identificativo Nazionale) is not a tax regime. It is the national STR license code required for stays under 30 days, enforced from 2025 with full compliance by March 2026 for existing hosts. Missing CIN triggers fines of €800 to €8,000 independent of whether you paid 21% cedolare secca correctly.

Tax choice still matters alongside CIN:

- A licensed first STR unit at 21% cedolare secca beats IRPEF for most high-income non-residents.
- Adding a second STR unit jumps that property to 26% cedolare, narrowing the gap to IRPEF if expenses are high on the second unit.
- Crossing three STR properties exits private cedolare entirely; compare business VAT net to portfolio IRPEF with a commercialista before scaling.

Municipal layers stack on top: Florence restricts new STR in UNESCO zones, Milan requires SCIA, Rome uses SUAR registration. Tourist taxes must be collected and remitted even when platforms withhold income tax. Full regulatory map: [short-term rental rules in Italy](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-italy/).

Display CIN on every listing and at the property entrance. Tax auditors and tourism inspectors cross-reference BDSR databases with RLI income lines.

## Which decision framework fits Cedolare Secca versus Irpef?

The decision turns on two variables, and neither is the property's running cost: the landlord's marginal IRPEF band, and whether there is other Italian income for personal allowances and renovation detrazioni to work against. Cedolare secca ring-fences the rent at a flat rate, which is worth having whenever the marginal band exceeds about 22% and worth checking whenever it does not.

Choose IRPEF when:

- The rent is most of your Italian income, so personal allowances still have room to work
- You are carrying renovation detrazione instalments that need IRPEF liability to offset
- Your only property is a concordato let and your total income falls inside the no-tax area
- Your commercialista models multi-year IRPEF on a furnished long-term lease with heavy mortgage interest
- You are scaling past three STR units and business taxation requires net-based planning anyway

Hybrid portfolios are common: cedolare secca on a stable Milan 4+4 tenant, IRPEF on a Tuscany STR in a heavy-expense renovation year via a newly registered contract. You cannot hybridize on the same registered lease mid-term.

## What are the pros and cons of cedolare secca versus IRPEF?

Cedolare secca offers foreign buyers certainty at 21% of gross rent and removes the 2% registration tax and stamp duty on the lease, at the cost of waiving ISTAT rent indexation for the term. Ordinary IRPEF offers no expense relief in return — 95% of the rent is taxed at your marginal band — so for a non-resident with no other Italian income to shelter, the flat rate is almost always the cheaper and simpler path.

A like-for-like comparison needs the same €850-1,100 monthly rent on both sides, 21% of gross against the marginal band applied to 95% of gross, and the regional and municipal surcharges of the commune where you are resident. IMU sits outside both calculations and belongs in the yield model, not the tax one.

| Path | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Cedolare secca | Fixed rate on gross rent; no lease registration tax or stamp duty; simple F24 payments; strong for anyone with other income | ISTAT indexation waived; 26% from the second STR unit; unavailable from the third |
| IRPEF | Deduct IMU, maintenance, interest; can beat 21% in low-income or capex years | Progressive rates up to 43%; heavier Modello Redditi PF filing; audit risk on thin invoices |

Choose cedolare secca when the rent stacks on other income, which is the ordinary case. Choose IRPEF only when the rent is most of your Italian income, or when renovation detrazione instalments need liability to absorb them.

## Underwriting snapshot

Underwriting this choice requires 3 inputs and no more: the gross annual rent, the landlord's other Italian taxable income, and the deductible costs the property genuinely produces. Foreign buyers who model only the 21% flat rate, without the IMU and spese that sit outside it, consistently overstate the net return.

This research screening (Q2 2026): among foreign buyers underwriting Florence, Milan, and Puglia rentals, the commonest first assumption is that IMU reduces the cedolare secca base. It does not. Most single-unit long-term landlords are better off retaining 21% cedolare secca; the exception is a year with heavy deductible renovation, where IRPEF can win. The other recurring error is underestimating the 26% rate that applies from a second short-let property.
