## How Does Greece's Golden Visa Compare with Italy's Investor Routes?

Golden Visa comparison typically means Greece granting five-year renewable residency from €250,000 qualifying property (higher in Athens, Mykonos, Santorini after August 2024 reforms), versus Italy requiring €250,000 in innovative startups or €500,000 in established companies with job creation, with no passive real-estate path that watch for for non-EU families prioritising Schengen access.....









| Program | Minimum investment | Asset type | Residency granted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greece Golden Visa | €250,000+ (area-dependent) | Residential or commercial RE | Yes, renewable |
| Italy investor visa | €250,000 startup / €500,000 company | Business equity | Yes, 2-year renewable |
| Italy elective residence | ~€31,000/year passive income | Not property-linked | Yes, annual renewal |

For non-EU families where EU residency is primary, Greece wins decisively. Buyers with EU passports treat residency as secondary and compete on asset quality. See [Italy investor visa property guide](/guides/italy-investor-visa-property/) for the active-investment alternative.

## How Do Property Taxes and Holding Costs Differ?

Tax comparison typically means Italy charging IMU at 0.86-1.06% plus IVAFE at 0.76% for non-residents, Greece applying ENFIA on assessed value generally lower on mid-tier stock, Italy offering 21% cedolare secca flat rent tax versus Greece progressive rates up to 44% for tax residents, structures that model with cross-border commercialisti before foreign buyers commit capital.....









| Charge | Italy | Greece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual property tax | IMU 0.86-1.06% | ENFIA (value-based) |
| Non-resident surcharge | IVAFE 0.76% | Withholding on rent |
| Rental income flat option | Cedolare 21% | No equivalent |
| Purchase tax (second home) | 9% registration | 3.09% transfer + VAT on new |

Golden Visa holders becoming Greek tax residents trigger worldwide income reporting. Italy's annual flat-tax regime for new residents — €300,000 for transfers from 1 January 2026 — appeals to a different HNWI segment.

### How do the two tax treatments compare?

Yield comparisons between the 2 countries collapse unless each is taken net of its own regime, because the deductions differ in kind rather than in degree. In Italy the relevant figures are fixed and published, which makes the Italian side of the comparison straightforward to model precisely.

| Line | Italy |
|---|---|
| Purchase, second home from a private seller | 9% of cadastral value |
| Annual ownership | IMU at 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value |
| Long-let income | 21% under cedolare secca, no deductions |
| Short-let income | 21% on the first unit, 26% on the second |
| Capital gains | Taxable on a sale within 5 years |

- Compare net against net; a gross yield computed on 2 different tax bases is not a comparison.
- Add the 10% to 15% all-in Italian entry cost before setting the 2 markets side by side.

## What Due Diligence Steps Apply in Both Markets?
Due diligence comparison typically means running parallel legal, tax, and technical checks before foreign buyers wire deposits in Italy or Greece, because both countries allow binding preliminary contracts that create exit costs if title or zoning fails later review.

| Step | Italy | Greece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title search | Notary visura + 20-year chain | Lawyer title report + cadastral |
| Building compliance | CILA/SCIA status | Building permit + energy cert |
| Tax ID | Codice fiscale | AFM registration |
| Reciprocity | Required for US/UK/AU | Open purchase for most nationalities |
| Typical timeline | 8-12 weeks | 6-10 weeks |

Commission independent surveyors on coastal stock in both markets because moisture, seismic, and illegal-extension risks differ by municipality but appear in both countries at similar rates on 1970s-1990s builds.

Cross-border buyers comparing Italy and Greece should budget €3,500-8,000 for combined legal, notarial, and technical due diligence before any deposit, regardless of headline price. Italy requires a notary-led deed with visura catastale, mortgage register clearance, and conformity certificates on renovations; Greek purchases lean on lawyer title reports, cadastral extracts, and building-permit verification with ENFIA tax history. Both markets expose foreign buyers to off-plan risk when developers market tourist licences that zoning never approved. Field teams report that 12-18% of island listings in Greece and 8-14% of coastal Puglia stock in Italy carry unresolved cadastral mismatches correctable pre-closing at €2,000-15,000. Stress-test transaction costs at 9-12% in Italy versus 8-11% in Greece including agent fees, notary, and registration before comparing net yields.

Foreign buyers comparing Italy and Greece rental yields in 2026 should model identical capital, hold period, and tax assumptions before choosing markets. Greece's advantage concentrates in Athens value districts where Koukaki and Kypseli apartments bought at €2,200-3,800 per sqm deliver 4.5-6% gross yields supported by expat and digital-nomad demand. Italy's national average sits at 4.3% per benchmark data, with Milan running 3-5%, Rome 2.5-5%, and Puglia plus Sicily exceeding 5% on coastal stock. Net yields in both countries typically fall 1.5-2.5 percentage points below gross after IMU or ENFIA, management at 15-25%, and rental tax. Italy offers 21% cedolare secca for non-resident landlords; Greece applies progressive taxation up to 44% for tax residents.
