## How do rental yields compare in Italy versus France?

Italy's national gross rental average sits near 4.3% against France's 3.5%. On a €500,000 apartment that difference is roughly €4,000 of additional annual rent before tax and management. The gap narrows or widens on the net line: Italy's cedolare secca fixes rental tax at 21% for long leases, while French rental income attracts income tax plus social charges. Compare after IMU, cedolare secca and condominium spese rather than on the gross figure.

1. Set Milan 3-5% gateway yields against Paris 2.5-3.5% before trophy allocation.
2. Screen Liguria 3-5% coastal bands before accepting Côte d'Azur yield compression.

Neither share means much on its own — run both cities on identical capital and hold-period assumptions before treating either as a liquidity signal.

1. Deploy more square metres per euro in Milan than Paris prime on identical capital.

3. Benchmark against STR registration friction before assuming Airbnb upside in either gateway city.

## How does the French IFI wealth tax compare with Italian IMU?

France taxes real-estate wealth directly: IFI applies from €1.3 million of net French property at sliding rates of 0.5-1.5%. Italy does not. A non-resident owner of Italian property pays IMU at 0.86-1.06% of cadastral value and nothing else on the holding, and cadastral values sit well below market prices. The gap widens as portfolio value rises, since IFI is charged on market value while IMU is not.

1. Model IFI from €1.3M net real-estate wealth before expanding French Riviera exposure.
2. Weigh French IFI on market value against Italian IMU on cadastral value, which lags it.
3. Run cross-border adviser models when rental income exceeds €40,000 gross per unit annually.

| Tax item | France | Italy |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Wealth tax trigger | €1.3M net RE | No net-wealth tax on RE |
| Annual property charge | Taxe foncière + IFI | IMU 0.86-1.06% |
| Non-resident surcharge | Social charges on rent | None beyond IMU |
| Rental income option | Progressive up to 45% | Cedolare secca 21% flat |
| Purchase tax (second home) | 5-6% droits de mutation | 9% registration tax |

Always model with a cross-border tax adviser. Treaty relief, domicile elections, and company structures change outcomes materially.

## What residency routes exist in each country?

Neither Italy nor France grants EU residency for a passive property purchase. Italy's investor visa requires €250,000 into an innovative startup or €500,000 into an established company, and a house counts toward neither. France offers talent and long-stay visas with no property route at all. Non-EU buyers who specifically want residency attached to real estate look to Greece, where Law 5162/2024 sets the threshold at €400,000, or €800,000 in the prime zones.

1. Treat property and residency as separate decisions in both markets during 2026 screening.
2. Budget 10-15% closing costs regardless of visa path chosen on €500,000 tickets.
3. Measure against Greece passive real-estate route only when residency is primary objective.

| Country | Property Golden Visa 2026 | Alternative route |
|---------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Greece | €400,000, or €800,000 in prime zones | Passive real estate qualifies |
| Italy | No | €250,000 startup visa |
| France | No | Talent and business visas |

For buyers who already hold EU access, Italy and France compete on yield, lifestyle, and tax rather than visa mechanics alone on €500,000-800,000 allocation tickets reviewed with cross-border counsel before compromesso.

## How does the purchase process differ for a foreign buyer?

EU citizens buy on identical terms in both countries. Non-EU buyers face open access in France but a reciprocity test in Italy, satisfied by treaty for US, UK, Canadian and Australian nationals. Costs and timelines differ: France's frais de notaire run about 7-8% on existing property with agency commission on top, against an Italian second-home stack of 9-11%. A French sale typically completes in three to four months, an Italian rogito in 60-120 days.

1. Order French diagnostics and Italian conformità review before price acceptance.
2. Model longer Italian urbanistica paths on heritage stock with abusi clearance risk.
3. Confirm reciprocity treaty coverage for non-EU nationality before offer submission.

| Step | France | Italy |
|------|--------|-------|
| Due diligence focus | Diagnostics, copropriété | Conformità, cadastral category |
| Typical timeline | 3-6 months | 4-9 months |
| Closing cost band | 10-13% | 9-11% |
| Non-EU access | Open | Reciprocity treaties |

France emphasises diagnostics (amiante, plomb, termites) and copropriété charges. Italy emphasises conformità catastale, abusi edilizi clearance, and cadastral category verification.
