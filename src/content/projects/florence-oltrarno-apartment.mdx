## What Is the Florence Oltrarno Apartment in Tuscany?

These are restored two-bedroom apartments listed through the Tranio marketplace in the UNESCO south-bank quarters around Santo Spirito, from about €400,000 for 60-90 sqm with terracotta floors and beamed ceilings, and 4-6% gross short-let potential where the licence position allows. The Oltrarno remains an artisan district rather than a tourist one, which is what distinguishes it for a buyer choosing walkable Florence over a Chianti agriturismo.

Oltrarno sits south of the Arno across Ponte Vecchio in Santo Spirito, San Frediano, and San Niccolò lanes where workshop culture and trattoria nightlife attract art tourists who book STR over north-bank hotel districts. Tranio portfolios list palazzo floors with heritage interiors and modern MEP upgrades behind protected facades.

## What Should You Know About Location and Area?

The Oltrarno puts Santo Spirito 5-15 minutes on foot, Ponte Vecchio 10-15, Santa Maria Novella station 20-30 and Peretola airport 25-35 by car. Identical floor area on the north bank costs 15-25% more, which is what makes the Oltrarno work for study-abroad and remote-worker leases.

- **Santo Spirito:** Evening wine bars drive STR bookings at €120-180 nightly peak
- **Artisan lanes:** San Frediano workshop culture supports premium listing photography
- **University demand:** US programs renew September leases at €1,200-1,800 monthly
- **UNESCO fabric:** Exterior changes restricted; Soprintendenza review runs 3-6 months

Oltrarno suits buyers wanting Florence centro access without €500,000+ north-bank entry on 60 sqm stock. Walkable services command 10-15% rent premiums versus car-only Chianti fringe contrada within 45 minutes of SMN.

## What Should You Know About Design and Units?

Typical stock is 60-90 sqm with terracotta floors, wooden beams and a modern kitchen fitted behind a heritage facade. Two premiums and one discount govern pricing: a terrace adds 20-30% where its conformità is clean, and a walk-up palazzo without a lift trades 15-25% below comparable stock — a discount that matters most to the buyers who will not accept third-floor access, which is a large share of the short-let market.

- **Standard 2-bed:** €400,000-700,000 on 60-90 sqm restored stock
- **Terrace premium:** €800,000-1,200,000 with legal rooftop or Boboli glimpses
- **Partial renovation:** €80,000-150,000 remediation budgets common on raw palazzo floors
- **Elevator access:** Material resale premium when ascensore serves upper floors

Illegal terrace enclosures remain frequent due diligence failures in agency listings. Buyers should treat absent conformità documentation as negotiation leverage before deposit.

IMU, 20-25% management fees and cedolare secca then compress the net — and Florence's licence freeze decides which route is even available.

An Oltrarno apartment is a scarcity asset whose income route depends on a licence rather than on demand. ## Can an Oltrarno Apartment Actually Be Let to Tourists?

Letting permission, not price, is what decides an Oltrarno case. New short-let licences are restricted inside the UNESCO centro, so a foreign buyer should treat permission as something to confirm in writing for the exact address rather than infer from a neighbouring listing.

- Confirm the comune's current position for the specific address before the caparra.
- Check the condominium regolamento separately: it can forbid tourist use regardless.
- If short lets are unavailable, model a twelve-month furnished lease to an academic or corporate tenant.

Cadastral plan gaps on subdivided palazzo stock are the other recurring finding. IMU runs 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value.
