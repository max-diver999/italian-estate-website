## What Is Costa Smeralda Apartment Stock?

Costa Smeralda apartment stock is sea-view and marina-adjacent bilocale from €600,000 across the Porto Rotondo, Romazzino and Liscia di Vacca corridors. Licensed short stays return 2-4% gross. Everything visible is governed by consorzio architectural control, and the resale market is German and Swiss rather than Italian.

Engel and Voelkers Gallura and syndicated portals market 1970s to 1990s consorzio-compliant residences with pool access and shared garden rights. ## What Should You Know About Location and Area?

The apartments sit in Arzachena comune: Porto Rotondo marina 5-15 minutes, Porto Cervo village 15-25, Pevero Golf 10-20, Olbia airport 35-50. What an apartment buys over a villa here is the removal of staffing overhead — nobody needs a gardener for a terrace — in exchange for walkable marina access.

Apartment stock in Porto Rotondo often trades 10 to 20 percent below Porto Cervo village per sqm while preserving yachting village lifestyle credentials attractive to European HNWI staging due diligence before villa upgrades. Summer SS125 traffic can add 30 to 45 minutes to Olbia airport transfers during peak regatta weekends when daily vehicle counts spike on coastal segments.

## What Should You Know About Design and Units?

Stock is consorzio-compliant residences built between the 1970s and 1990s: €600,000-900,000 at entry, €900,000-1,500,000 for premium top-floor terraces, usually with pool and shared garden rights. Terrace enclosures must follow the consorzio palette, and any visible exterior work takes 3-6 months of geometra review.

Consorzio Costa Smeralda architectural controls apply to exterior colours, roof forms, and terrace additions on renovations. Buyers planning enclosure upgrades should commission geometra pre-checks against approved consorzio palettes before compromesso deposits because fines for unauthorised awnings or paint deviations can delay resale marketing when previous owners ignored rules visible from coastal roads.

## What Is the Real Season on Costa Smeralda?

Season length is what separates the headline rate from the annual return here. Foreign buyers on a €600,000 apartment should underwrite roughly 3 months of genuine demand, because Gallura's letting market concentrates almost entirely into high summer while standing costs run for the full 12.

| Line | Reality |
|---|---|
| Genuine season | Roughly 3 months |
| Off-season | IMU, spese and utilities continue |
| Service charges | High on resort-managed stock |
| CIN registration | Required before advertising tourist use |

- Model annual occupancy rather than August rate; the difference usually decides whether the case works.
- Ask for the resort or consortium service charge in writing before the caparra, not after.
- Short-let income is taxed at 21% on the first unit and 26% on the second.
