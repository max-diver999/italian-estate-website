## What Does Owning Cave Stock Actually Involve?

Sassi property is rock-cut, and that gives it obligations ordinary apartments do not have. Humidity management and structural monitoring are continuing costs rather than one-off items at purchase, and any visible alteration requires heritage clearance before works can lawfully begin.

- Humidity control is inherent to cave dwellings, not a sign of a bad unit.
- Structural monitoring is ongoing; a single survey at purchase is not sufficient.
- Confirm the heritage position on planned works before the caparra, not after.

Sassi stock cannot be replicated, so value rests on tourism demand and what the authority permits. IMU runs 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value.

Owning inside the Sassi is closer to custodianship of protected fabric than to apartment ownership, and the obligations are continuing rather than one-off. Humidity is the defining issue: rock does not breathe like masonry, so mechanical ventilation and dehumidification run year-round, and remediation on a conversion budgets at €15,000-35,000 before finishes. Structural monitoring applies to the cave itself. Any exterior alteration needs Soprintendenza approval, at €3,000-8,000 of heritage consultancy and 8-12 week timelines. Access is on foot down stepped lanes, which shapes both letting and refurbishment logistics. Against that sits a UNESCO inscription with no European equivalent, €2,060 per sqm city averages and 4-6% gross on licensed short-let. Buyers who price the maintenance honestly do well here; those who price it as an apartment do not.
