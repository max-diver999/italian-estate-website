## What Is Chianti Farmhouse Restoration Stock?

Chianti restoration stock is stone casali across Greve, Radda, Gaiole and Castellina, typically €300,000 to acquire and €150,000-500,000 to restore, on footprints of 150-250 sqm. Licensed hospitality returns 4-6% gross. The resale market is American and British wine tourism, which is deeper here than anywhere else in rural Italy.

Engel & Voelkers and Gate-away portfolios mix turnkey restored casali with raw shells needing roof, timber beam, and MEP remediation behind Classico landscape vincolo. Each contrada carries independent agricultural land category, pool SCIA history, and agriturismo eligibility that buyers must verify before compromesso on vineyard-view marketing photography.

## What Should You Know About Location and Area?

The four Chianti communes behave differently. Greve carries the wine-tourism footfall, Radda the highest nightly rates, Gaiole the cheapest entry, Castellina the producer concentration. All sit 30-50 minutes from Florence and 30-45 from Siena, and a typical 180 sqm farmhouse here is close enough to be reachable and far enough that the silence is real.

April-October wine event calendar drives vendemmia harvest experiences and shoulder occupancy independent of beach tourism. Properties with documented private parking and SS222 lane access outperform walk-only casali on guest review logistics during Greve festival weekends when daily vehicle counts spike on Chianti Classico routes.

Greve commands strongest restaurant and wine-shop footfall while Radda upsell positioning supports 15 to 20 percent per sqm premiums over Gaiole rural stock on comparable 180 sqm casali in Chianti comparables. SS222 harvest traffic adds 20 to 40 minutes to Florence airport transfers during October vendemmia weekends when contractors and tourists share hill lanes.

## What Should You Know About Design and Units?

Stone façades and terracotta roofs are not stylistic choices here; they are permit conditions. Restored 150-250 sqm layouts run €500,000-900,000 turnkey, raw shells €300,000-600,000 plus €250,000-600,000 of remediation. A pool needs its own SCIA on a compliant plot, and visible exterior work takes 3-6 months of landscape review.

Inventory spans two-bedroom casali on 2 to 5 hectare olive parcels to four-bedroom farmhouses with guest annex potential marketed for agriturismo conversion. Buyers should treat infinity pool renders as conditional until geometra confirms vincolo paesaggistico clearance and agricultural land category supports hospitality bed counts on filed SCIA records.

## What Should You Know About Investment Case?

A licensed agriturismo on €700,000-1,100,000 of total capital returns 5-6% gross; the same house let short-term without the agriturismo licence returns 4-5%. April to October carries 60-70% of the year. Resale takes 18-30 months, so the difference between the two routes compounds over a long hold.

| --- | --- |

| STR gross (restored casale) | 4 to 5 percent |
| Apr-Oct revenue share | 60 to 70 percent of annual gross |
| Renovation cost per sqm | 800 to 1,500 EUR |
| Management (agriturismo) | 20 to 30 percent of gross |

Model agriturismo only when commercialista confirms documented vineyard or olive production meeting commune minimum hectares. STR on restored casali without agricultural activity suits buyers accepting 4 to 5 percent gross with simpler cedolare secca filing at 21 percent on furnished stays.

Transaction stack on 800,000 EUR total invested capital runs 10 to 12 percent including registration tax, notary, structural survey, and heritage consultancy. Agriturismo launch adds 40,000 to 70,000 EUR for compliant kitchen, guest furniture, and agricultural registration before first harvest-season guest week.

Non-resident buyers rarely secure Italian mortgage leverage above 50 percent LTV on rural casali because banks underwrite agricultural land risk slowly. Cash or cross-border mortgage buyers dominate Chianti restoration transactions, which speeds rogito to 90 to 120 days when conformità and land registry packets are complete pre-compromesso.

A Chianti farmhouse is bought by an international pool and sold to the same one, which is what makes the case work despite modest yields. Restored casali run from €400,000 past €1 million; licensed agriturismo and short-let return 4-7% gross on a season that starts earlier and ends later than Puglia's, because the demand is cultural rather than coastal. Resale is the strongest in rural Italy, with correctly priced Chianti stock clearing in 6 to 12 months and the region drawing 14.77% of all international enquiries for Italian property. The risks are execution rather than demand: restoration priced from a listing rather than a survey, and landscape vincoli that catch a pool or a terrace after purchase. Model a 7 to 10 year hold with the works fully costed and the case is sound.

## Who Is This For?

Three buyers make this work. Wine-tourism operators who can absorb 12-24 months of renovation before any revenue; American and British buyers wanting a base between Florence and Siena; and portfolio investors offsetting countryside illiquidity with something urban, such as a Florence Oltrarno apartment.

- **Agriturismo operator:** Documented vineyard or olive production with licensed hospitality beds
- **STR investor:** Restored casali in Greve corridor without agricultural compliance overhead
- **Lifestyle renovator:** Long hold with selective summer rental when not in personal use
- **Tuscany ladder buyer:** Countryside step-up after Florence or Siena urban entry

Against: total capital from €500,000, landscape permit delays, genuinely complex agricultural licensing, and contractor lead times of 12-18 months before a first booking.

Five checks before compromesso on a €300,000-1,200,000 ticket. The agricultural land category; a structural survey on any abandoned casale; agriturismo eligibility confirmed with a commercialista rather than assumed; pool SCIA conformità where one exists; and a restoration budget carrying 15-20% contingency rather than none.

| Structural risk | Engineer survey on roof beams and foundations |

## What Should a Chianti Restoration Budget Cover?

Restoration capex typically exceeds the purchase saving on Chianti farmhouse stock. Foreign buyers at around €300,000 are usually acquiring a partially restored casale where roof, services and seismic works are outstanding, and where the agricultural parcel attached to the house carries its own rules.

| Line | What to establish before offer |
|---|---|
| Roof and structure | Survey before caparra; the single largest variable |
| Services | Rural connections are frequently absent or undersized |
| Land classification | Agricultural parcels restrict further building |
| Agriturismo licensing | Separate path if letting is the plan |

- Have a geometra price the works against the cadastral plan rather than the listing photographs.
- If letting is the intention, sequence the licence path before committing the capex.
- Budget IMU at 0.86% to 1.06% of cadastral value annually on a second home.

A Chianti restoration budget has four components and listings routinely quote only the first of them. Structure comes first: roof, beams and stone repair on a building that has usually been unoccupied for years, which is where surveys produce the largest surprises. Mechanical and electrical follows — heating, plumbing and wiring brought to modern standards behind a facade that cannot be altered. Then the external works: terracing, the access track, and a pool if the landscape vincolo permits one where it would be visible from a protected viewpoint. Finally the professional layer, with Soprintendenza clearance adding 8-12 weeks before a contractor can mobilise and a geometra needed throughout the programme. On a €400,000-1M farmhouse, budgeting from a builder's estimate rather than from a survey is the single most common way a Chianti project overruns its figure.
