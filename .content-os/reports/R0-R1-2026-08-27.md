## What R0 turned out to be

The plan called for stripping eight shared national blocks from fifty pages. For
the twelve nationality pages that happened inside R1, because they were rewritten
rather than edited. What R0 actually needed was the other half: the pages those
twelve now link to.

Two of them were themselves broken and scoring zero.

`how-to-buy-italy-property-step-by-step` had 17 sections, 11 of which restated
their own heading, 11% self-repetition and two gates. Most of its subjects
already had owners elsewhere. It now holds one thing nobody else does: which step
blocks which, why the bank's 6 to 10 week valuation cannot start before the
compromesso exists, why the seller's planning file sets the deed date, and what
each broken link costs to repair.

`italy-property-by-nationality-guide` was a summary of the twelve country pages
plus the shared Italian blocks. It now holds the single thing Italian law asks
about nationality, article 16 reciprocity read from citizenship rather than
residence, including how a partial table entry restricts a rural purchase and how
the test differs for a company.

## The gate that had to change, and why it was a tightening

`validate:batch` enforced GEO 90 from `scripts/lib/geo-citability-scorer.mjs`.
That rubric scores provably templated text 92.0 and hand-written articles 93.1,
so a wave whose whole purpose is removing the template failed its own gate. This
was not a threshold to negotiate but a broken instrument.

The gate now scores with the calibrated rubric at a floor of 34, which is the
lowest score the twenty hand-written pages on this site actually reach in
production. Lower number, higher standard: `areas/ancona` and the old
`italy-property-for-germans` both passed the old gate and both fail the new one
at 0 of 75.

## Self-collision, measured and then fixed

The failure this wave was most at risk of is writing twelve pages in one sitting
and reproducing the corpus's duplication in miniature. Measured across the twelve
against each other alone, the first pass produced 3.2% worst-pair duplication and
35 shared nine-word sequences between the Australian and British pages.

The collisions were exactly where the playbook says to look: the closing
paragraph of links, and the sentence stating the Schengen stay limit. Both were
reworded page by page rather than exempted. After that pass the worst pair is
2.3% and the largest shared count is 27.

## A detector bug found by the writing

The malformed-token gate fired on the UAE page for "it It". Neither word was
doubled: a list item ended in "to it" and the next section opened "It converts",
and `plainText` removes the heading between them. The repeated-word arm was
matching case-insensitively across a sentence boundary.

The rule is now case-sensitive on the repetition, since a real templating artefact
is "the the" and never "it It". Measured on the labelled sets the change costs
nothing: it still fires on 5 of 46 machine files with 49 tokens, and on 0 of 12
hand-written and 0 of 7 middle files. Calibration holds at 70.8 points.
