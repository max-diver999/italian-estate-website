## The rule that decides every page

**Every page must hold a subject no neighbouring page holds.**

Not reworded — held. The test is whether a reader who has just read the
neighbouring page learns something new and specific here. Measured on this
corpus: a section named "Overview" or "Investment potential" duplicates its
neighbours by 30-100%; a section named for a concrete local mechanism duplicates
by 0-5%.

Each thesis below was drafted by reading the page, then put through a collision
pass against every other thesis in the plan *before* anything was written. That
pass moved fifteen subjects between pages and rejected four outright. It also
turned up four facts worth knowing before any of this starts:

- **`concessione demaniale` already appears on eight or more pages.** The Como
  and Bellagio theses are the ninth and tenth rendering of the corpus's
  most-duplicated subject, so they only work if the national explanation moves to
  one owner first.
- **46 files already say "seismic".** Any new disaster-origin thesis lands in a
  crowded field.
- **`canone concordato` is already owned twice nationally**, on
  `guides/cedolare-secca-italy-rental-property` and
  `compare/cedolare-secca-vs-irpef-italy-rental`.
- Confirmed absent corpus-wide: **Campi Flegrei / bradisismo — zero hits**, and
  **Salva Casa / DL 69/2024 — zero hits in all 48 area pages and all 30
  comparisons.** Those are the two genuinely unowned subjects on the site.

## R0 — the prerequisite, before any page below is rewritten

Twelve nationality pages, twenty-five area pages and thirteen comparisons share
eight blocks of text that are **facts about Italy**, not about any of them:
registration tax, the closing-cost stack, IMU, cedolare secca, mortgage LTV, the
five-phase purchase process, the seven-item due-diligence list, and the four
"buyer scenario" archetypes.

Each already has a national owner (`italy-registration-tax-property`,
`imu-property-tax-italy`, `cedolare-secca-italy-rental-property`,
`non-resident-mortgage-italy`, `cost-of-buying-property-italy`,
`how-to-buy-italy-property-step-by-step`, `due-diligence-italy-property`).

R0 strips those blocks from the fifty pages and links to the owner instead.
**Without R0 the shared mass survives and no thesis separates anything** — the
pages would be rewritten and still collide. This is roughly a day of work and it
is the single highest-value change in the plan.

`how-to-buy-italy-property-step-by-step` is itself in the worst 15 (openers 7 of
20, two gates) and is one of the owners, so it gets rewritten inside R0 rather
than left as a broken destination.

## How each wave runs

1. Written by hand, one page at a time. No repeated construction between pages of
   the same wave — including source lines and closing paragraphs.
2. After each page: `node scripts/geo-score.mjs <file> --explain` plus the content
   validator.
3. **After the wave, re-check the wave against itself.** Writing three pages in
   one sitting reproduces the original duplication in miniature; that is a
   measured failure mode on the reference site, not a hypothetical.
4. Commit per wave with before → after per file.
5. Push to `claude/italian-estate-content-audit-8zgre9`.

Gates before every push: `npm run validate:content` · `npm run facts:review` ·
`npm run geo:calibrate` · `npm run build`. All four green, and the text gets
shortened rather than the gate loosened.

## What success looks like

R0-R3 covers 50 of the 109 zero-scoring pages. If they land where the
hand-written set lands, corpus mean moves from 15.6 to roughly 27-30 and the
zero count roughly halves. The reference site went from 30.8 to 53.5 across four
waves, and it started from a corpus twice as healthy as this one.
